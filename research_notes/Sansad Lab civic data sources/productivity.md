# Indian Parliament Productivity Statistics (14th-17th Lok Sabha; 18th Lok Sabha by session)

Research date: 2026-10-27. Primary source is PRS Legislative Research (prsindia.org). Most PRS web pages carry no visible publication date (only a "Copyright 2026" footer), so the dates below are session-end dates, PDF metadata, or press dates. PRS charts are embedded images that could not be read, so several historical cells are gaps.

PRS method note: PRS "productivity" means time actually functioned divided by scheduled time. It can go above 100% when sittings run late. Government and Secretariat figures (PIB, Parliamentary Affairs Ministry, Lok Sabha Secretariat) use a different calculation and usually come out higher in disrupted sessions. See the discrepancies under Q2.

## Q1. Number of sittings per Lok Sabha / per session

### Takeaway
The 17th Lok Sabha held 274 sittings, fewer than any earlier full-term Lok Sabha. The 16th held 331. The 18th Lok Sabha sessions found so far range from 15 sittings (Winter 2025) to 31 (Budget 2026).

### Cited Findings
- 17th LS (Jun 2019-Feb 2024): 274 sittings. Only four earlier Lok Sabhas had fewer, and all four were dissolved early. 40 scheduled sittings (13%) did not take place. Report dated Feb 10, 2024 (PDF metadata). Average sittings per year fell to 55, against 135 in the 1st LS. — [PRS, Functioning of the 17th Lok Sabha](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha); [PDF](https://prsindia.org/files/parliament/vital_stats/Functioning-17th_Lok_Sabha.pdf); [Vision IAS summary, Feb 13, 2024](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2024-02-13/polity-and-governance/prs-released-vital-stats-on-functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- 16th LS (2014-19): 331 sitting days. PRS gives an average of 468 sitting days for full-term Lok Sabhas. PRS media update dated Feb 2019. — [PRS, Functioning of 16th Lok Sabha](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019); [PRS media updates Feb 2019](https://prsindia.org/media/media-updates/2019/feb)
- 18th LS, Budget Session 2025 (Jan 31-Apr 4, 2025): both Houses functioned for 26 days. — [PRS Vital Stats BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025)
- 18th LS, Monsoon Session 2025 (Jul 21-Aug 21, 2025): both Houses sat on all 21 scheduled days. Note dated Aug 21, 2025. — [PRS Vital Stats MS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-monsoon-session-2025)
- 18th LS, Winter Session 2025 (Dec 1-19, 2025): both Houses sat for 15 days. — [PRS Vital Stats WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)
- 18th LS, Budget Session 2026 (Jan 28-Apr 18, 2026): both Houses functioned for 31 days against 30 planned. The session was due to end Apr 2 and was extended for the delimitation Bills. — [PRS Vital Stats BS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2026/vital-stats)
- 18th LS, Monsoon Session 2026 (Jul 20-Aug 13, 2026): both Houses met for 19 days. — [PRS Vital Stats MS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/vital-stats)
- Winter Session 2024 was scheduled for Nov 25-Dec 20, 2024. — [PRS Plan vs Performance WS 2024](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/winter-session-2024/plan-performance)

### Inferences
- The 17th LS sat on 57 fewer days than the 16th (331 - 274), a drop of about 17%. Part of the cause is the COVID-shortened sessions of 2020-21.

### Gaps
- Sittings for the 14th and 15th LS: PRS shows these only in image charts I could not read. Commonly cited values (about 332 and about 345) are not verified, so they are left out.
- Sittings for the First Session 2024 (Jun 24-Jul 3, 2024), Budget Session 2024 (Jul 22-Aug 9, 2024) and Winter Session 2024: not retrieved. Check https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2024/vital-stats and https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/winter-session-2024/plan-performance.
- No 18th LS session after Monsoon 2026 was found. Winter Session 2026 had not started as of the research date.

## Q2. Productivity (% of scheduled time worked), Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha

### Takeaway
On PRS figures, the 17th LS worked 88% of scheduled time and the Rajya Sabha 73%. Across the 18th LS, productivity has swung from over 140% (Budget 2024) to a low of 15% in the Lok Sabha (Monsoon 2026), which PRS-cited press calls the lowest since Winter 2016. Government figures are consistently higher than PRS figures in disrupted sessions.

### Cited Findings
**Full terms (PRS):**
- 14th LS: 13% of scheduled time lost to disruptions in the Lok Sabha, 14% in the Rajya Sabha, which implies about 87% and 86% functioning. — [PRS 16th LS page](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- 15th LS: 37% lost in the Lok Sabha and 32% in the Rajya Sabha, implying about 63% and 68%. — [same](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- 16th LS: 16% lost in the Lok Sabha and 36% in the Rajya Sabha, implying about 84% and 64%. — [same](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- 17th LS: Lok Sabha 88%, Rajya Sabha 73%. Dated Feb 10, 2024. — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)

**18th LS sessions (PRS unless noted):**

| Session | Dates | LS % | RS % | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Budget Session 2024 | Jul-Aug 2024 | 148% | 137% (counting actual sitting days only) | [PRS plan-performance BS 2024](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2024/plan-performance) (search snippet; page not fully read) |

**Discrepancies with official figures:**
- Winter 2024: the Lok Sabha Secretariat put the Lok Sabha at about 57.87% (62 hours) and the Rajya Sabha at 40.03% (43h27m). Minister Rijiju said "over 54%" for the Lok Sabha and 40% for the Rajya Sabha (Dec 20, 2024). PRS gives 52% and 39%. — [AIR/Newsonair Dec 20, 2024](https://newsonair.gov.in/lok-sabha-registers-54-productivity-rajya-sabha-at-40-during-winter-session/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fparliament-winter-session-dhankhar-calls-for-serious-reflection-after-disruptions-kill-productivity-3326834)
- Monsoon 2026: the Parliamentary Affairs Ministry (Rijiju) gave 19% for the Lok Sabha and 39% for the Rajya Sabha. PRS gives 15% and 33%. Press dated Aug 13-14, 2026. PRS-cited press says this is the lowest Lok Sabha figure since Winter 2016. For comparison, Winter 2010 was worse, at 5% in the Lok Sabha and 2% in the Rajya Sabha. — [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/parliament-productivity-at-19-in-lok-sabha-39-in-rajya-sabha-what-it-means-for-indian-democracy); [Bhaskar English](https://english.bhaskardigital.com/parliament-session-ends-lok-sabha-productivity-falls-to-15/); [Vision IAS / ET, Aug 14, 2026](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-08-14/the-economic-times/polity-and-governance/lok-sabhas-monsoon-session-productivity-among-lowest-in-22-years-prs-data)
- PRS described Monsoon 2025 as the lowest functioning of the 18th LS at that point. Monsoon 2026 then went lower. — [PRS MS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-monsoon-session-2025)

### Inferences
- The 15th LS (2009-14) was the most disrupted of the four full terms on PRS data.
- The 17th LS had high productivity on PRS's time measure (88%), but low sittings and low committee scrutiny. Productivity % alone overstates how well it functioned.

### Gaps
- PRS's direct "% functioned" figures for the 14th-16th LS. I derived them as 100 minus % time lost, so treat them as approximate.
- The Budget 2024 and Winter 2024 PRS figures come from search snippets of PRS pages and were not fully re-fetched.
- Rajya Sabha figure for Budget 2025 (the fetched summary attributed the 111% to the Lok Sabha).
- Budget 2026: no official (government) figure was found.

## Q3. Number of bills passed per Lok Sabha / session

### Takeaway
The 17th LS passed 179 Bills and the 16th LS 133, excluding Finance and Appropriation Bills. The 18th LS has been light on legislation: PRS says its first year passed 11 Bills, the fewest since 1999.

### Cited Findings
- 17th LS: 179 Bills passed (excluding Finance and Appropriation Bills). 58% were passed within two weeks of introduction. — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- 16th LS: 133 Bills passed, which PRS says is 15% more than the previous Lok Sabha. — [PRS 16th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- Budget Session 2025: 10 Bills passed (including the Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024) and 5 introduced. PRS headline: "Eleven bills passed in the first year of 18th Lok Sabha, lowest since 1999." — [PRS VS BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025)
- Monsoon Session 2025: 12 Bills passed by the Lok Sabha and 14 by the Rajya Sabha (press). PRS notes several were passed without discussion. — [search result, Tribune newsbytes](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/newsbytes-145); [PRS MS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-monsoon-session-2025)
- Winter Session 2025: 9 introduced, 7 passed (including the nuclear energy regulator, MGNREGS and insurance laws), and 2 referred to Committees. — [PRS VS WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)
- Budget Session 2026: 10 introduced and 6 passed (other than 2 Appropriation Bills and 1 Finance Bill). The Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill on delimitation and women's reservation was defeated: 298 for and 230 against, short of the special majority. Two linked Bills became infructuous. — [PRS VS BS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2026/vital-stats); [PRS bill-legislation BS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2026/bill-legislation)
- Monsoon Session 2026: press counts conflict, between 11 and 12 Bills passed. Minister Rijiju said 12 by both Houses. Nine were passed in the Lok Sabha with no MP other than the Minister speaking. PRS gives no total on the page I read. — [Business Standard, Aug 14, 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/parliament-monsoon-session-ends-with-12-bills-passed-amid-disruptions-126081400121_1.html); [Outlook (11 bills)](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/monsoon-session-concludes-with-11-bills-passed-lok-sabha-productivity-at-19); [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/india/12-bills-passed-11-without-debate-parliaments-monsoon-session-ends-at-19-lok-sabha-productivity)

### Inferences
- From PRS's "15% more", the 15th LS passed about 116 Bills (133 / 1.15). This is derived and not directly sourced.

### Gaps
- Bills passed by the 14th and 15th LS (directly sourced figures).
- Bills passed in the 2024 sessions. The "11 in the first year" figure implies about 1 Bill across the 2024 sessions before Budget 2025, but this is unverified.

## Q4. % of bills referred to parliamentary committees per Lok Sabha

### Takeaway
PRS's final figures are 60% (14th), 71% (15th), 25% (16th) and 16% (17th). The brief's 27% for the 16th LS is a mid-term or secondary figure, not PRS's final number. In the 18th LS, 11 of 42 Bills (about 26%) had been referred as of Winter 2025.

### Cited Findings
- 14th LS 60%, 15th LS 71%, 16th LS 25%. — [PRS 16th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- 16th LS figures vary across sources: 27% (study guide), 28%, and 29% (a PRS note from 2017, mid-term). — [Shankar IAS](https://shankariasparliament.com/blogs/pdf/referring-a-bill-to-parliamentary-standing-committees); [PRS Parliament Diary, Apr 12, 2017](https://prsindia.org/media/media-updates/prs-parliament-diary-budget-session-ii-session-wrap-apr-12th-2017)
- 17th LS: 16%, lower than the previous three Lok Sabhas. — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- 18th LS: 4 of the first 20 Bills had been referred by Budget 2025. By Winter 2025 it was 11 of 42 introduced. These include 2 on simultaneous elections and 3 on removal of ministers on detention, all with Joint Committees. Only the Securities Markets Code had gone to a Standing Committee. — [PRS BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025); [PRS WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)

### Inferences
- The 18th LS's roughly 26% referral rate as of Dec 2025 is above the 17th LS's 16%, but most of the referrals went to ad hoc Joint or Select Committees rather than Departmentally Related Standing Committees.

### Gaps
- The denominator differs across PRS notes (Bills introduced vs Bills passed). PRS figures generally use Bills introduced.

## Q5. Average time spent on bills; Question Hour functioning

### Takeaway
Debate on Bills became shorter over the 17th LS: 35% of Bills passed with under an hour of Lok Sabha discussion. Question Hour has been badly disrupted in the 18th LS, falling to 1% of scheduled time in the Lok Sabha in Monsoon 2026.

### Cited Findings
- 17th LS: 35% of Bills passed after less than 1 hour of Lok Sabha discussion (Rajya Sabha 34%). Question Hour ran for 60% of scheduled time in the Lok Sabha and 52% in the Rajya Sabha. 24% of listed oral questions were answered in the Lok Sabha and 31% in the Rajya Sabha. — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- Share of Bills discussed for over 3 hours: 14% (14th), 22% (15th), 32% (16th). Bills passed within 30 minutes: 26% (15th), 6% (16th). The 16th LS spent 32% of its time on legislative business, against an average of 25% for other Lok Sabhas, and 13% on Question Hour. — [PRS 16th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-16th-lok-sabha-2014-2019)
- Question Hour in 18th LS sessions:
  - First Session 2024: no Question Hour was held.
  - Budget 2025: Lok Sabha 78%, Rajya Sabha 83%. Ministers answered 28% of starred questions orally. — [PRS BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025)
  - Monsoon 2025: Lok Sabha 23%, Rajya Sabha 6%. — [PRS MS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-monsoon-session-2025)
  - Winter 2025: Lok Sabha 67%, Rajya Sabha 80%. 23% (Lok Sabha) and 26% (Rajya Sabha) of starred questions were answered orally. — [PRS WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)
  - Monsoon 2026: Lok Sabha 1%, Rajya Sabha 12%. In the Lok Sabha, Question Hour ran for about 9 minutes in the whole session, and 2 of 380 listed questions were answered orally. — [PRS MS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/vital-stats); [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/parliament-ends-monsoon-session-with-decade-low-productivity)
- Budget 2025: the Waqf Bill was debated for 13h53m in the Lok Sabha and 12h49m in the Rajya Sabha (which sat until 4:02 am on Apr 3). The Budget got 49 hours in total, and about 90% of budgeted expenditure was passed without discussion. — [PRS BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025)
- Budget 2026: 3 Bills (delimitation and women's reservation) were discussed for over 21 hours across two days. — [PRS BS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2026/vital-stats)

### Gaps
- Average hours per Bill as a single figure for each Lok Sabha. PRS reports distributions (<1 hour, >3 hours) instead.
- Question Hour % for the 14th and 15th LS, and for the 2024 sessions other than the First Session.

## Q6. Notable records

### Takeaway
The 17th LS set several negative records: fewest sittings for a full term, no Deputy Speaker for the whole term, and mass suspensions. The 18th LS has still not elected a Deputy Speaker, and Monsoon 2026 was its least productive session.

### Cited Findings
- The 17th LS held the fewest sittings of any full-term Lok Sabha (274). Only four Lok Sabhas sat less, all dissolved early. — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- The 17th LS was the first Lok Sabha to go its entire term without electing a Deputy Speaker (Article 93). PRS notes there has been no Deputy Speaker since June 2019, which continues into the 18th LS (stated in the Budget 2025 and Winter 2025 notes). — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha); [PRS WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)
- Suspensions: 206 instances across both Houses in the 17th LS. 146 MPs were suspended in Winter Session 2023 (Dec 2023). — [PRS 17th LS](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/functioning-of-the-17th-lok-sabha)
- The 18th LS passed 11 Bills in its first year, the fewest since 1999. — [PRS BS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-budget-session-2025)
- Budget 2026: a constitutional amendment (131st, on delimitation and women's reservation) was voted down, 298 to 230. — [PRS BS 2026 search summary](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/budget-session-2026/vital-stats)
- Monsoon 2026: Lok Sabha productivity of 15% was the lowest since Winter 2016 (press citing PRS, Aug 2026). In the Lok Sabha, Private Members' Bills were introduced in Winter 2025 for the first time since Aug 2024. — [Bhaskar English](https://english.bhaskardigital.com/parliament-session-ends-lok-sabha-productivity-falls-to-15/); [PRS WS 2025](https://prsindia.org/parliamenttrack/vital-stats/parliament-functioning-in-winter-session-2025)

### Gaps
- How the 146 suspended MPs split between the Houses (widely reported as 100 Lok Sabha and 46 Rajya Sabha) was not verified in a fetched source.
- Exact PRS publication dates for most web pages. Only the 17th LS PDF (Feb 10, 2024) and the Monsoon 2025 note (Aug 21, 2025) have confirmed dates.
