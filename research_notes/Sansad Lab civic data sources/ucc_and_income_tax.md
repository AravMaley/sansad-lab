# SUBJECT A — A Uniform Civil Code for India

## A0. Framing note for the scenario (important)

Parliament has **never** introduced, let alone passed, a national UCC bill, so the scenario is genuinely counterfactual at the Union level. But as of October 2026 it is **no longer hypothetical at the state level**: four states have passed UCC legislation and at least one more is drafting. The scenario should say so plainly, otherwise it will read as out of date. See §A3.

The constitutional hook matters: Article 44 sits in Part IV (Directive Principles), which **Article 37 expressly makes non-justiciable** — "shall not be enforceable by any court". So Article 44 is a direction to the State, not a command a court can enforce. ([Business Standard explainer on Article 37/44](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/ucc-state-laws-legal-challenge-uniform-civil-code-article-44-254-126091800789_1.html))

Also worth flagging for balance: personal law is in the **Concurrent List** (List III, Entry 5 — marriage, divorce, infants, adoption, wills, succession, joint family, partition), which is why states can legislate on it and why Article 254 repugnancy and presidential assent are live issues.

## A1. Constitutional basis — Article 44 and the Constituent Assembly

### Exact text [VERIFIED]

> **Article 44. Uniform civil code for the citizens.** — The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India.

Source: [constitutionofindia.net — Article 44](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-44-uniform-civil-code-for-the-citizens/)

### The debate [VERIFIED]

- Draft number: **Draft Article 35** of the Draft Constitution of 1948. Draft text was identical except it read "for citizens" rather than "for the citizens".
- Debated and **adopted on 23 November 1948**, the same day, without amendment.
- Source: [constitutionofindia.net](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-44-uniform-civil-code-for-the-citizens/); full transcript at [Indian Kanoon — Constituent Assembly Debates, 23 November 1948](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/870715/)

### The amendments moved by Muslim members [VERIFIED, via the debate transcript]

All were **negatived**:

| Member | Substance of amendment | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Mohamad Ismail Sahib** (Madras) | Proviso: any group, section or community of people shall not be obliged to give up its own personal law where it has one | Negatived |
| **Naziruddin Ahmad** (West Bengal) | Proviso: personal law guaranteed by statute shall not be changed "except with the previous approval of the community", as ascertained by the Union Legislature | Negatived |
| **Mahboob Ali Baig Sahib Bahadur** (Madras) | Proviso: "nothing in this article shall affect the personal law of the citizen" | Not separately recorded in the extract; not carried |
| **B. Pocker Sahib Bahadur** (Madras) | Supported Ismail; argued personal laws had been protected even under British rule | — |
| **Hussain Imam** (Bihar) | Argued India's diversity made a uniform code impractical "for the present"; urged safeguards | No separate amendment recorded |

Source: [Indian Kanoon — CAD 23 Nov 1948](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/870715/); summary cross-checked at [ThePrint, "How Ambedkar, Munshi & Krishnaswamy Ayyar argued for UCC at Constituent Assembly"](https://theprint.in/india/how-ambedkar-munshi-krishnaswamy-ayyar-argued-for-uniform-civil-code-at-constituent-assembly/771945/) and [CLPR, "UCC Part 1 — Constitutional History"](https://clpr.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/UCC-Part-1-Constitutional-History.pdf)

### For the article, in the Assembly [VERIFIED]

- **K.M. Munshi**: argued religion should be confined to its proper sphere, and that personal law should not be treated as religion by default; a UCC was needed for national unity and for women's rights.
- **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**: a uniform code would promote national unity — "There is no use clinging always to the past."

### Ambedkar's reply — the key nuance for the scenario [VERIFIED]

Ambedkar **declined all the amendments**, but his defence was notably narrow and two of his points cut both ways:

1. He pointed out India already had a near-uniform civil code in almost every area **except** marriage and succession — so the article was not inventing uniformity from nothing.
2. He noted Muslim personal law had **not** been uniform across India historically (he cited the North-West Frontier Province and parts of the United Provinces, Central Provinces and Bombay where Hindu law of succession had applied to Muslims until the 1937 Shariat Act).
3. Crucially, he said the article **does not require the code to be imposed on all citizens "merely because they are citizens."** He suggested a future Parliament might make the code **purely voluntary in the first instance** — applying only to those who declare they are willing to be bound by it — and cited the **Shariat Act, 1937** itself as a precedent for that opt-in technique.

Source: [Indian Kanoon — CAD 23 Nov 1948](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/870715/)

> Note for the scenario writers: point (3) is genuinely used by **both** sides today. Pro-UCC advocates cite Ambedkar's defence of the article; anti-UCC advocates cite the same speech to argue Ambedkar never contemplated a compulsory code. Present it whole.

## A2. What a UCC would replace — the existing personal-law statutes

Plain-language summary. Statute texts are on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/).

| Statute | Who it covers | What it governs, in plain terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Hindu Marriage Act, 1955** | Hindus, and by statutory definition Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs | Conditions for a valid marriage (monogamy, age 18/21, prohibited degrees), ceremonies, registration, restitution of conjugal rights, judicial separation, grounds for divorce, divorce by mutual consent, maintenance pending suit |
| **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** (amended 2005) | Same group | Who inherits when someone dies without a will. The **2005 amendment** made daughters coparceners in Hindu Undivided Family property by birth, equal to sons — the single biggest gender-equality reform in Indian personal law to date |
| **Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937** | Muslims | A short Act. It does not codify Muslim law; it **directs courts to apply Muslim personal law** (rather than custom or usage) to Muslims in listed matters — succession, marriage, dissolution, maintenance, dower, guardianship, gifts, trusts, wakfs. Uncodified law is then found in classical fiqh and precedent |
| **Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872** | Marriages where at least one party is Christian | Who may solemnise a Christian marriage, licensing of ministers, notice, registration. Divorce for Christians is separately under the **Indian Divorce Act, 1869** (substantially amended 2001) |
| **Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936** | Parsi Zoroastrians | Marriage validity, registration, and divorce — notably tried by special **Parsi Matrimonial Courts** sitting with delegates (a jury-like panel), a procedure unique in Indian family law |
| **Special Marriage Act, 1954** | **Anyone, any religion or none** | The existing **opt-in secular route**. A civil marriage with no religious ceremony, available for inter-faith and inter-caste couples. Succession for those who marry under it is governed by the Indian Succession Act, 1925, not personal law |

### The Special Marriage Act point cuts both ways — flag this in the scenario
- **Against a UCC**: a secular civil code already exists and is voluntary; anyone who wants out of personal law can already use it. The honest question is whether it should be *compulsory*.
- **For a UCC**: the SMA's **30-day public notice** requirement (ss. 5–7) publicly exposes inter-faith couples to family and vigilante pressure, which is why uptake is low. The 2018 Law Commission paper recommended reforming exactly this. So "there's already an opt-out" is weaker than it looks.

Source for the SMA notice recommendation: [LiveLaw on the 2018 consultation paper](https://www.livelaw.in/law-commission-says-ucc-neither-necessary-nor-desirable-at-this-stage/)

### A3.1 Uttarakhand — Uniform Civil Code of Uttarakhand Act, 2024

**Legislative record**
- Drafted by a five-member expert committee chaired by **Justice (retd.) Ranjana Prakash Desai**, former Supreme Court judge. [SINGLE SOURCE for the chair — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/ucc-state-laws-legal-challenge-uniform-civil-code-article-44-254-126091800789_1.html); widely reported elsewhere but I did not get a second clean citation]
- **Passed by the Uttarakhand Legislative Assembly on 7 February 2024**, after two days of debate. [VERIFIED]

These are reconcilable (a voice vote in a House where BJP held ~47 of 70 seats and the opposition did not force a division), but **do not quote a division count of 70–0 as if a division was taken** — I could not find the Assembly record. Treat as unresolved.
- **[CONFLICT] Presidential assent**: reported as **11 March 2024** by some explainers and **12 March 2024** by [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2024/12/18/269920/bharat/uniform-civil-code-will-be-implemented-in-uttarakhand-from-january-2025-cm-dhami/amp/) (which may be the notification rather than assent date). Act No. 01 of 2024. Verify against the Uttarakhand Gazette before publishing a date.
- **Came into force 27 January 2025**, by notification, alongside the Uniform Civil Code Uttarakhand Rules, 2025. Uttarakhand became the first Indian state to implement a UCC. [VERIFIED — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1078380/uttarakhand-to-implement-uniform-civil-code-on-january-27); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/01/india-uttarakhand-becomes-first-state-to-implement-the-uniform-civil-code/); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/uttarakhand-becomes-first-state-to-implement-uniform-civil-code/)]

**What it actually does** [VERIFIED — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Civil_Code_of_Uttarakhand_Act,_2024); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/02/06/del117-ldall-ucc.html); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/01/30/uttrakhand-government-became-first-state-to-implement-ucc/)]

- **Marriage**: common minimum age — 18 for women, 21 for men, for all communities. **Registration of all marriages mandatory.**
- **Divorce**: one uniform set of grounds for all communities (cruelty, adultery, desertion, mental illness, etc.). Maintenance for spouse and children.
- **Polygamy banned** for everyone. **Halala and iddat prohibited**; **triple talaq** prohibited.
- **Inheritance**: sons and daughters inherit equally; the distinction between ancestral and self-acquired property is removed; a single succession framework regardless of religion.
- **Live-in relationships**: **registration is mandatory**, with penal consequences for failure. Children of registered live-in partners are legitimate with inheritance rights; a deserted partner may claim maintenance.
- **Scheduled Tribes are exempt** and may continue under customary law (~2.9% of the state's population).

**[CONFLICT] Live-in penalty**: [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/ucc-bill-uttarakhand-live-in-relationship-enn24020603971) reports three months' imprisonment for failing to register within a month; [BoomLive](https://www.boomlive.in/news/uttarakhands-ucc-what-live-in-partners-must-declare-27651), reporting on the Rules, cites six months. Check the enacted sections (the Act has distinct penalties for non-registration, for late registration, and for false statements, which is probably the source of the confusion).

**How it has worked since** [VERIFIED, figures as of 19 January 2026 — one-year mark]
- **4,74,447 marriages registered** in the first year. Registrations averaging **~1,400/day**, against ~67/day under the previous registration Act. Near-100% done online.
- **68 live-in relationships registered**; **2 live-in termination certificates** issued.
- **316 divorce/dissolution certificates** issued online.
- Sources: [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/announcements/one-year-of-ucc-online-marriage-registration-transforms-process-over-47-lakh-marriages-registered-in-uttarakhand); [The Shillong Times](https://theshillongtimes.com/2026/01/19/1-year-of-ucc-about-5-lakh-marriages-registered-in-uttarakhand-online-services-expedite-process/); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/within-year-of-implementation-of-uniform-civil-code-in-uttarakhand-nearly-5-lakh-people-applied-under-ucc-enn26011502373)

> This is the single most useful "what actually happened" fact for the scenario: **the marriage-registration machinery worked at scale; the live-in registration provision — the most controversial part — was used 68 times in a year.** That is a strong, neutral, empirical finding that does not favour either side and should be the centrepiece of the reveal.

### A3.2 Goa — the Portuguese-era civil code [VERIFIED in outline, [CONFLICT] on detail]

- Goa applies the **Portuguese Civil Code of 1867**, extended to the overseas provinces by a Decree of 18 November 1869 with effect from **1 July 1870**. After Goa's accession to India in 1961, Indian laws were extended to the territory **except family law**, which remained under the Portuguese Code. ([Wikipedia — Goa Civil Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa_Civil_Code); [University of Lisbon Faculty of Law, "Family Law in Goa after the Act of 2012"](https://www.fd.ulisboa.pt/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Goa-FAMILY-LAW-IN-GOA-AFTER-THE-ACT-OF-2012.pdf))
- **It is not perfectly uniform.** Carve-outs survive — notably the **Codes of Usages and Customs of the Gentile Hindus of Goa, 1880**, which created exceptions in family and succession law for Hindus, including a limited allowance for a Hindu man to take a second wife in defined circumstances (failure to produce a male heir by a certain age). Catholic marriages solemnised in church had, under the 1940 Concordat, a distinct status. ([University of Lisbon paper](https://www.fd.ulisboa.pt/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Goa-FAMILY-LAW-IN-GOA-AFTER-THE-ACT-OF-2012.pdf); [The Leaflet, "Patriarchy and property rights: Goa's 'Uniform Civil Code'"](https://theleaflet.in/equality/patriarchy-and-property-rights-goas-uniform-civil-code))
- The **Goa Succession, Special Notaries and Inventory Proceeding Act, 2012** (succession provisions brought into force 2016) partially re-codified the field in Indian statutory form.
- Chief Minister **Pramod Sawant** has said the Goa code can be a model for other states. ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/goa-civil-code-can-be-model-for-states-seeking-to-implement-ucc-cm-pramod-sawant-392999))

> Balance note: the Supreme Court in 2019 called Goa "a shining example" (§A5), but feminist legal scholars argue Goa's code embeds patriarchy — the communion-of-assets regime is real gender progress on property, while the Hindu bigamy exception and some succession rules are not. Present both.

**The Article 254 problem** — a good, non-partisan "what actually happened" point. Because personal law is in the Concurrent List, state UCCs that conflict with Union personal-law statutes need **presidential assent under Article 254(2)** to prevail in that state, and even then Parliament can override. The result is **multiple non-identical "uniform" codes**, which is an awkward outcome for both camps. ([Business Standard, "One Uniform Civil Code, many state versions: The legal challenge ahead", 18 September 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/ucc-state-laws-legal-challenge-uniform-civil-code-article-44-254-126091800789_1.html))

## A4. The Law Commission

### 21st Law Commission — Consultation Paper on Reform of Family Law, 31 August 2018

- Chaired by **Justice (retd.) B.S. Chauhan**. Issued on **31 August 2018**, the day the Commission's term ended. It is a **consultation paper, not a report** — the 21st Commission never submitted a final report on the UCC. [VERIFIED]
- **The exact wording** [VERIFIED]:

> "This Commission has therefore dealt with laws that are discriminatory rather than providing a uniform civil code which is neither necessary nor desirable at this stage."

- Other key passages [VERIFIED]:
  - "Resolution of this conflict does not mean the abolition of difference."
  - "Most countries are now moving towards recognition of difference, and the mere existence of difference does not imply discrimination, but is indicative of a robust democracy."
  - Reforms should be made in a way that "does not compromise the diversity and plurality that constitutes the core of India's social fabric."
- **Its alternative**: amend and further codify the personal laws of each community — especially succession and inheritance — to remove gender discrimination; fix gaps in the **Special Marriage Act, 1954** and the **Guardians and Wards Act, 1890**; make provisions gender-neutral. It deliberately left polygamy, nikah halala and adultery largely alone as matters then pending before the Supreme Court.
- Sources: [LiveLaw, 1 September 2018](https://www.livelaw.in/law-commission-says-ucc-neither-necessary-nor-desirable-at-this-stage/); [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/uniform-civil-code-is-neither-necessary-nor-desirable-at-this-stage-21st%C2%A0law-commission-said-in-2018-518224); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/08/31/uniform-civil-code-necessary-now-law-commission.amp.html). Paper PDF is on the Law Commission site but is a **scanned image PDF** and not machine-readable — I could not extract text directly from it. The quoted wording above is taken from LiveLaw's contemporaneous report and corroborated by The Tribune.

## A5. Supreme Court judgments

| Case | Citation / date | What it held, and what it said about a UCC |
|---|---|---|

The two dissenting judges would have left it to Parliament |
| **Ahmedabad Women Action Group v. Union of India** | (1997) 3 SCC 573 | **The counterweight case.** The Court declined to examine the constitutionality of personal laws, holding that reform of personal law is a matter of **legislative policy for Parliament**, not for the courts. Essential for balance — the Court has *urged* a UCC repeatedly but has never *directed* one, and has expressly said it will not |

Sources: [India Legal, "Uniform Civil Code: A Shining Example"](https://indialegallive.com/constitutional-law-news/supreme-court-news/uniform-civil-code-a-shining-example/); [Morung Express, "An executive prerogative: Where the Supreme Court stands on UCC"](https://morungexpress.com/an-executive-prerogative-where-the-supreme-court-stands-on-ucc); [Wikipedia — Sarla Mudgal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarla_Mudgal_and_Others_v._Union_of_India)

## A6. Arguments FOR — attributed

1. **Equality before the law regardless of religion.**
   **Amit Shah, Union Home Minister**, in the **Rajya Sabha, December 2024**: the BJP will bring a UCC in every state it governs, as in Uttarakhand — "if nation and state are secular, how can laws be based on religion?" ([NewsOnAir, 18 December 2024](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/bjp-to-bring-uniform-civil-code-in-every-state-hm-amit-shah))

2. **The existing system is itself discriminatory — reframed as "secular" vs "communal".**
   **Prime Minister Narendra Modi**, **Independence Day address from the Red Fort, 15 August 2024**: a large section of the country believes the civil code they live under is "communal"; a **"secular civil code"** is "the need of the hour" to end discrimination on the basis of religion. ([Business Today, 15 August 2024](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/current-civil-code-is-communal-secular-ucc-is-need-of-the-hour-pm-modi-from-red-fort-441574-2024-08-15); [Daily Pioneer](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2024/top-stories/pm-modi-calls-for-secular-civil-code--says-existing-laws-discriminatory.html))

3. **Gender justice within personal laws — from within the affected community.**
   **Zakia Soman, co-founder, Bharatiya Muslim Mahila Andolan (BMMA)**, in BMMA's submission to the Law Commission: "In our submission, we want affirmative aspects to be retained like consent before marriage, Mehr … and discriminatory practices like polygamy, Nikah Halala / Muta and denial of custody to go away." BMMA's national survey of **4,700+ Muslim women** found **90% wanted polygamy ended**. Soman separately called the invalidation of polygamy a "welcome feature." ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/bharatiya-muslim-mahila-andolan-advocates-gender-justice-for-muslim-women-in-ucc-submission); [IndiaSpend](https://www.indiaspend.com/governance/1-step-forward-many-steps-back-in-uttarakhands-uniform-civil-code-900242))
   *This is the most important "for" attribution to include — it is a Muslim women's organisation, not a party, and it prevents the scenario reading as BJP-vs-minorities.*

4. **Article 44's direction, and the founders' intent.**
   **Dr B.R. Ambedkar**, Constituent Assembly, **23 November 1948**: a near-uniform civil code already covered almost every field except marriage and succession, and Muslim personal law had itself never been uniform across India. ([CAD 23 Nov 1948](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/870715/))
   **K.M. Munshi**, same sitting: religion should be confined to its proper sphere and personal law should not automatically be treated as religion.
   **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**, same sitting: a uniform code promotes national unity — "There is no use clinging always to the past."

5. **The courts have asked, repeatedly, for sixty years.**
   **CJI Y.V. Chandrachud**, *Shah Bano*, **23 April 1985**: Article 44 has "remained a dead letter."
   **Justice Kuldip Singh**, *Sarla Mudgal*, **10 May 1995**: directed the Government to report what steps it had taken.
   **Justices Deepak Gupta and Aniruddha Bose**, *Jose Paulo Coutinho*, **13 September 2019**: Goa is "a shining example."

6. **Simplifying a fragmented legal system.**
   The Uttarakhand experience is the empirical case: a single online registration regime took marriage registrations from ~67/day to ~1,400/day with near-total digital uptake. ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/announcements/one-year-of-ucc-online-marriage-registration-transforms-process-over-47-lakh-marriages-registered-in-uttarakhand))
   **Pramod Sawant, Chief Minister of Goa**: the Goa code can be a model for other states. ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/goa-civil-code-can-be-model-for-states-seeking-to-implement-ucc-cm-pramod-sawant-392999))

## A7. Arguments AGAINST — attributed

1. **Religious freedom under Articles 25–26.**
   **Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind**, resolution passed at its working committee, **2023**: the UCC is against religious freedom and against the Constitution; the organisation would not protest on the streets but would "take all possible legal steps." President **Maulana Arshad Madani** said the matter affects all Indians, not only Muslims. In its submission to the Law Commission it called a mandatory UCC an attempt to enforce one identity over diverse identities, and a threat to legal pluralism. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/ucc-against-religious-freedom-will-oppose-says-jamiat-ulema-e-hind-1229104.html); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1051197/uniform-civil-code-is-against-the-constitution-says-jamiat-ulama-e-hind); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/jamiat-ulema-e-hind-opposes-ucc-calls-it-a-threat-to-legal-pluralism-in-its-submission-to-law-commission-panel))
   **Asaduddin Owaisi, AIMIM president**, on the Uttarakhand Act: it is "an attack" on minorities and violates **Articles 25 and 29**. ([Wikipedia — Uttarakhand UCC Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Civil_Code_of_Uttarakhand_Act,_2024))

2. **Minority fears of majoritarianism — that "uniform" will mean "Hindu".**
   **Flavia Agnes, women's-rights lawyer and co-founder of Majlis**, in *The Wire*: the aim should be "uniformity of rights, not a uniform law." Her argument is that Hindu law was historically the *most* gender-unjust — daughters could not inherit, widows had only a limited life estate, women had no right to divorce, and there was no restraint on male polygamy — and was reformed only in the 1950s–2005. "It is far better to reform personal laws and ensure that laws of all communities are gender-just rather than enacting a UCC." She has also said the UCC is "raked up for Muslim bashing and to serve electoral needs." ([The Wire interview](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/interview-flavia-agnes-says-the-aim-should-be-the-uniformity-of-rights-rather-than-the-ucc); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/rights/ucc-bogey-muslim-bashing-electoral-needs-flavia-agnes))
   The **Uttarakhand Mahila Morcha** and others criticised the Uttarakhand Act for following "the Hindu law template" and for omitting protective features of Muslim law such as compulsory **mahr**. ([NewsClick](https://www.newsclick.in/how-gender-just-uttarakhand-uniform-civil-code))

3. **Tribal communities, whose customary law is constitutionally protected — including objections from BJP allies.**
   - **Neiphiu Rio, then Chief Minister of Nagaland**: said Home Minister **Amit Shah** assured him the UCC would **not apply to Scheduled Tribes** of Nagaland and the rest of the North-East. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/nagaland/amit-shah-assured-ucc-will-not-be-applicable-for-sts-in-the-northeast-nagaland-cm-neiphiu-rio-2648747))
   - **NDPP (Nationalist Democratic Progressive Party) — a BJP ally in Nagaland**: implementing the UCC "will have a negative impact on the freedom and rights of the minority communities and the tribal people of India." The **NPF** opposed it too. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/bjps-ruling-coalition-partner-in-nagaland-opposes-ucc-1232480.html); [Sentinel Assam](https://www.sentinelassam.com/north-east-india-news/nagaland-news/customary-laws-constitutional-provisions-negate-need-for-uniform-civil-code-ne-parties-657542))
   - **Nagaland Tribal Council**: a UCC would dilute **Article 371A**, which protects Naga religious and social practices, customary law and land ownership.
   - **Conrad Sangma, Chief Minister of Meghalaya and NPP president** (then an NDA partner): the UCC "in its present form" goes against the spirit and the idea of India. ([EastMojo](https://eastmojo.com/assam/2023/07/05/is-northeast-india-united-in-its-opposition-to-uniform-civil-code/); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1043505/explained-why-some-north-eastern-states-are-opposing-the-uniform-civil-code))
   - **Zoramthanga, then Chief Minister of Mizoram** (MNF, then an NDA partner): the UCC conflicts with Mizoram's customary laws. The **Mizoram Assembly passed a resolution on 14 February 2023** opposing any step towards enacting a UCC. ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/india/ucc-mizoram-cm-opposes-uniform-civil-code-bjp-ally-northeast-nda-law-commission-pm-modi-latest-updates-2023-07-04-879341))
   - **AIADMK**, a BJP ally in Tamil Nadu, said it would urge the Centre not to bring constitutional amendments for a UCC that adversely affect minorities' religious rights. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/bjp-ally-aiadmk-not-in-favour-of-uniform-civil-code-1234217.html))
   - **Suhas Chakma, Rights and Risks Analysis Group**: a UCC would require constitutional amendment, including to **Article 13(3)(a)** (which gives "custom or usage" the force of law), and North-East exemptions would raise the question of extending the same to Fifth Schedule tribal areas. ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1043505/explained-why-some-north-eastern-states-are-opposing-the-uniform-civil-code))

   *Note the structural point: every state UCC so far exempts Scheduled Tribes, including Uttarakhand. A "uniform" code that exempts ~8.6% of the national population by birth is, on its face, not uniform — a fair observation for the scenario that belongs to neither camp.*

4. **The 2018 Law Commission's view: reform each personal law rather than replace all of them.**
   **21st Law Commission (Chair: Justice B.S. Chauhan), 31 August 2018** — see §A4 for the exact wording. The Commission's recommended alternative was targeted, community-by-community reform to remove gender discrimination, plus fixes to the Special Marriage Act.
   **K.C. Venugopal, AICC general secretary**: Congress agrees with the 2018 Law Commission finding. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/national/national-politics/more-opposition-parties-join-the-anti-ucc-chorus-1232370.html))

5. **The practical difficulty of codifying hundreds of customs.**
   **Prof. Faizan Mustafa**, constitutional scholar (then Vice-Chancellor, NALSAR), **2018**: a UCC cannot be adopted "in one go"; reform should be piecemeal; all communities' laws need reform or the issue "will be hijacked by fanatics of all religions"; uniformity is not itself a value — gender-sensitive law is. ([Hindustan Times, 1 September 2018, via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-delhi/20180901/281681140751657))
   The Constituent Assembly version of the same argument: **Hussain Imam**, 23 November 1948 — India's scale and diversity made a uniform code impractical at that time.

6. **Civil-liberties objection to how a UCC has actually been implemented.**
   The Uttarakhand live-in registration regime drew privacy challenges from **Senior Advocate Raju Ramachandran** and **Advocate Vrinda Grover** (see §A3.1), and the state itself subsequently amended the Rules to drop Aadhaar requirements, pregnancy reporting and police surveillance — effectively conceding part of the objection. This is the strongest *evidence-based* argument against, as distinct from the in-principle ones. ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/north/uttarakhand/uttarakhand-revises-ucc-norms-on-live-in-and-marriage-registration-212129))

## B2. What it does

### Replacement and scope [VERIFIED]
Replaces the **Income-tax Act, 1961** in its entirety from **1 April 2026**.

### Simplification figures
| Metric | 1961 Act | 2025 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Sections | **819** (per Sitharaman in the Lok Sabha; the Act had 298 numbered sections but ~819 effective provisions once lettered insertions like 115BAC are counted) | **536** |
| Chapters | 47 | **23** |
| Words | ~5.12 lakh | ~2.6 lakh (roughly halved) |
| Schedules | 14 | **16** |

**[CONFLICT]** — Deccan Herald reported the old Act as "298 sections," which contradicts the 819 figure used by the Finance Minister and most other reporting. Both are defensible (298 numbered vs 819 effective); the civic tool should explain the discrepancy rather than pick one silently.
Sources: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/income-tax-bill-2025-introduced-in-lok-sabha-promises-simplicity-125021400741_1.html); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/new-income-tax-bill-to-have-622-pages-536-sections-likely-to-be-tabled-in-parliament-on-feb-13); [Tribune — Panda interview](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/new-income-tax-bill-halves-word-count-eases-compliance-chairman-of-the-finance-select-committee-baijayant-jay-panda/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_2025)

### Drafting method
Provisos and explanations largely removed in favour of **tables and formulae**; redundant and spent provisions deleted; cross-references rationalised. ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/income-tax-bill-2025-introduced-in-lok-sabha-promises-simplicity-125021400741_1.html))

### "Tax year" [VERIFIED]
The 2025 Act abolishes the twin concepts of **"previous year"** (the year income is earned) and **"assessment year"** (the following year in which it is assessed) and replaces both with a single **"tax year"**. This is the change most visible to ordinary taxpayers and the easiest to explain in the scenario.
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_2025); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-income-tax-no2-bill-2025))

### What actually changed substantively vs what was only redrafted — the crucial distinction

**Only redrafted (per the government's own position):**
The Finance Ministry's three stated guiding principles were (1) textual and structural simplification, (2) continuity and certainty with **no major tax policy changes**, and (3) **no modification of tax rates**. The Select Committee recorded that "the Bill mainly seeks to simplify the language, and does not aim to bring any substantive changes." Tax rates and regimes for individuals and corporates, most definitions, offences, penalties and the administrative structure all carry over. ([PRS Select Committee summary](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/prs-products/prs-select-committee-report-summary-1753279747); [Business Today — Panda](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/new-income-tax-bill-2025-simplifies-law-keeps-policy-intact-baijayant-panda-486447-2025-07-26))

**Genuinely new or changed** [VERIFIED, per PRS]:
1. **Faceless schemes**: the Act replaces the 1961 Act's specific faceless provisions (inquiry/valuation, revision of orders, collection/recovery) with a **general power for the central government to frame schemes**, which must be laid before Parliament. This is a shift of detail from statute to executive rule-making.
2. **Virtual digital space in searches** — see §B3.
3. **Undisclosed income in search cases** now expressly includes **virtual digital assets** alongside money, bullion and jewellery.
4. **Tax treaty interpretation**: a treaty term undefined in the treaty, the Act or a central notification takes the meaning given in **any other central law**.
5. **Dispute Resolution Panel**: must now give its directions **together with the points of determination and reasons**.

### Slabs and the rebate — these belong to the Finance Act, not this Act

Important for accuracy. The headline **₹12 lakh effectively tax-free** figure and the revised slabs were announced in the **Union Budget on 1 February 2025** and enacted through the **Finance Act, 2025**, which amended **s.115BAC and s.87A of the 1961 Act**. The Income-tax Act, 2025 simply **carries them forward** into its own numbering:

- The default new regime (old **s.115BAC**) becomes **s.202** of the 2025 Act, with rates in the relevant Schedule: **nil up to ₹4 lakh**, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and **30% above ₹24 lakh**.
- The rebate (old **s.87A**): a resident individual with total income up to **₹12,00,000** gets a rebate of the whole tax or **₹60,000**, whichever is less.
- **[CONFLICT] which section holds the rebate.** Several sources say **s.156**; others say **s.202**. Verify against the Act text before publishing.
- Sources: [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/section-202-section-115bac-tax-regime-income-tax-act-2025.html); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-act-2025); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/new-tax-regime-section-115-bac-becomes-section-202-in-the-new-tax-bill-slabs-rates-details-here/article-146273/)

> **The scenario should say clearly: this Act did not cut anyone's taxes.** The 2025 tax cut was the Finance Act. Conflating them is the single most common error in public coverage.

### Select Committee's main recommendations [VERIFIED — PRS]
Chair **Baijayant Panda**; report tabled **21 July 2025**; **285+ recommendations**, all accepted by the Finance Ministry. The substantive ones were largely restorative — putting back things the drafters had dropped:
- Restore the deduction for **inter-corporate dividends** passed on by companies on the concessional rate.
- Redraft **associate enterprise** provisions and the rules on setting off long-term capital losses against short-term gains, to match the 1961 Act.
- Narrow the **investment fund** definition; fix the "inland vessels"/"new inland vessels" confusion.
- Restore **nil TDS / zero tax liability** provision for senior citizens, for parity with the 1961 Act.
- Move references to phasing-out incentives (infrastructure companies, certain hotels and convention centres, SEZs) into **savings clauses**.
- Add definitions: "parent company", "cooperative bank", "beneficial owner" for employer pension contributions.
- Incorporate the **Finance Acts 2024 and 2025** changes, including removal of the **equalisation levy** on specified services.
- Large volume of pure drafting fixes: wrong and missing cross-references, redundant text, typographical errors.
- Separately, the Committee recommended retaining **CAs as the sole professionals authorised to conduct tax audits**, which drew objections from **CMAs and CSs**.
Source: [PRS Select Committee Report Summary](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/prs-products/prs-select-committee-report-summary-1753279747); [ICAI presentation](https://icaitv.com/files/1766136318Income_Tax_Act_2025.pdf)

## B3. The controversy — "virtual digital space" and search powers

### Section number [VERIFIED]
**Section 247** of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (clause 247 in the Bill) — the search-and-seizure power, successor to **s.132 of the 1961 Act**. The definitions sit in **s.261** (clause 261 in the Bill).

### What was proposed [VERIFIED]
In the **February 2025 Bill**:
- **Cl. 247(1)(i)** — enter and search any building, place, vessel, vehicle or aircraft where the officer suspects relevant books, documents, money, bullion, jewellery, virtual digital assets or **data stored on remote servers / cloud platforms** are kept.
- **Cl. 247(1)(ii)** — require the person to provide **reasonable technical assistance, including access codes/passwords**; refusal is non-compliance.
- **Cl. 247(1)(iii)** — authorise **forced access**: "break open the lock … or **override the access code to any computer system or virtual digital space**, where the access code thereof is not available."
- **Cl. 247(1)(vii)** — seize computers, laptops, servers, storage devices.
- **Cl. 261(e)** — defines **"computer system"**, and **"virtual digital space"** is defined to include email servers, **social media accounts**, online trading/investment accounts, banking accounts, cloud storage, digital application platforms and websites storing ownership details of assets.
- **Cl. 249** — bars disclosure of the officer's recorded "reason to believe"/"reason to suspect" to any person or authority (including the Appellate Tribunal), and applies **BNSS, 2023** search provisions.

Source: [Internet Freedom Foundation letter to the Select Committee, 10 March 2025](https://internetfreedom.in/iff-writes-to-the-select-committee-to-review-the-digital-search-and-seizure-powers-under-the-income-tax-bill-2025/); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-income-tax-bill-2025)

### What was recommended
Stakeholders asked the Select Committee to require officers to **record tangible written reasons** before accessing digital spaces, and some asked that **social media accounts be excluded** from search and seizure altogether.
**[CONFLICT] what the Committee did.** Some reports say the Committee **cleared the provision without substantial change**, reaffirming access to digital platforms and password override. Others describe a review. The PRS summary of the report does **not** list any recommendation on s.247, which is consistent with "no change recommended." Treat "the Select Committee left the search power essentially intact" as the probable position but flag it as not fully confirmed.
Source: [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/digital-privacy-issues-under-income-tax-act-2025-search-powers-explained-1440688/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/union-budget/biz-income-tax-bill-virtual-3404985)

### What survived into the Act [VERIFIED, with nuance — this is subtle and the scenario must get it right]
In the **enacted Act**, **s.247(1)(b)(iii)** no longer names "virtual digital space" separately. It permits an officer to **"override the access code to any computer system"** where the access code is unavailable. However, **s.261(e) defines "computer system" to include "virtual digital space."**

So: the explicit standalone reference was removed, but the substance is retained **through the definition**. Describing the power as "removed" (as some tax commentary does) is misleading; describing it as "unchanged" is also not quite right. The accurate formulation is: **the drafting was tidied; the reach was not narrowed.**
Source: [Taxscan — "Digital Privacy Issues under Income Tax Act, 2025: Search Powers Explained"](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/digital-privacy-issues-under-income-tax-act-2025-search-powers-explained-1440688/)

### The privacy objections [VERIFIED]

**Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF)**, letter to the Select Committee, published **10 March 2025**:
- It is unclear how **zero-knowledge and end-to-end encrypted systems** would be treated under the compelled-assistance provision.
- Digital spaces unconnected to the search — shared tools, group messaging — could be swept in without the affected third party getting a reasonable opportunity to respond.
- **Mere suspicion** of undisclosed income suffices to trigger an intrusive digital search, and authorisation is **internal** (senior tax officials), not judicial.
- Neither the 1961 Act nor the Bill requires authorities to **preserve digital evidence securely** — no hash-value validation or chain-of-custody integrity safeguards.
- **Puttaswamy proportionality**: *K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India* (2017) made privacy a fundamental right, so *M.P. Sharma* (1954) and *Pooran Mal* (1974) — which upheld search powers against fundamental-rights challenges — can no longer settle the question. Any infringement must be "justifiable, necessary, and minimally invasive."
Source: [IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/iff-writes-to-the-select-committee-to-review-the-digital-search-and-seizure-powers-under-the-income-tax-bill-2025/)

**ICAI (Institute of Chartered Accountants of India)**, pre-Budget memorandum, reported **29 January 2026** — notable because this is the accounting profession's own body, *after* enactment:
- Flagged **s.247** as allowing **excessive access to electronic communications and personal data**; warned the sweeping powers could be misused and **"may not withstand constitutional scrutiny."**
Source: [Business Today, 29 January 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/budget-2026-icai-warns-new-income-tax-act-gives-tax-officials-sweeping-access-to-personal-digital-data-513569-2026-01-29)

**Government/defender position**: tax authorities and some commentators called the claim that the department can enter personal email or social media accounts without consent "false and misleading," noting the powers only operate within an authorised search where the officer has reason to believe undisclosed income or assets exist. ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/digital-privacy-issues-under-income-tax-act-2025-search-powers-explained-1440688/); [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2025/07/22/303888/bharat/can-income-tax-officials-demand-your-passwords-heres-what-the-new-2025-bill-proposes/))

### Other criticism
- **Passage without debate** — see §B4.
- **Delegation**: replacing specific faceless-assessment provisions with a general scheme-making power moves substance from statute to executive notification.
- **Tax-audit monopoly**: CMA and CS bodies objected to the Committee's recommendation that only CAs may conduct tax audits.
- **Readiness**: a parliamentary committee flagged IT infrastructure risk, citing past portal glitches, and urged extensive testing of the ~₹1,200 crore digital overhaul, plus faster notification of rules and forms. ([Whalesbook summary](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/economy/India-Tax-Law-Launch-Faces-Delays-IT-Glitches-Ahead-of-April-1-Deadline/69b3b81ef962c451ea5fa528))

### Court challenge [VERIFIED in substance]
**Vishwaprasad Alva v. Union of India** — a PIL under **Article 32** in the **Supreme Court**, filed by entrepreneur **Vishwaprasad Alva**, challenging **s.247 of the 2025 Act** and the corresponding **s.132 of the 1961 Act**.

- **Counsel**: Senior Advocate **Sanjay Hegde**, with AoR **Pranjal Kishore**.
- **Bench**: **CJI Surya Kant**, Justice **Joymalya Bagchi**, Justice **N.V. Anjaria**.
- **Heard**: around **early February 2026** (reported 10 February 2026).
- **Grounds**: s.247 permits search of "computer systems" including phones, laptops, cloud storage and "virtual digital space"; searches may be authorised on a belief that a person "will not" or "would not" comply in future — an **"anticipatory search" framework** failing proportionality; authorisation is internal, without prior judicial approval; non-disclosure of "reasons to believe" blocks meaningful review; *Puttaswamy* (2017) requires stronger safeguards for digital data, and *Pooran Mal* (1974) cannot settle the question post-*Puttaswamy*.
- **Court's response at the preliminary hearing**: not persuaded the provisions confer "unguided or uncanalised" power; the CJI said the power was not "uncontrolled" because statutory preconditions exist. Justice Bagchi: "if you find that there is no rational nexus between the material before the department and the reasonable belief recorded by them in their records, the entire procedure will be set aside." The Bench said anticipatory powers help prevent destruction of digital evidence and that concerns are better addressed by fact-specific judicial scrutiny. **No interim relief.** The petitioner was directed to examine *Principal Director of Income Tax (Investigation) v. Laljibhai Kanjibhai Mandalia* (2022), on limited judicial review of "reasons to believe."
- **Outcome**: the Supreme Court ultimately **refused to entertain** the petition, saying the petitioner could approach the authorities with a representation seeking clarification of the law. Reported in LiveLaw's **direct tax digest for March 2026** as "Supreme Court Refuses To Entertain Plea Challenging Search Powers Over Digital Devices Under Income Tax Act, 1961, And 2025 Act."
- **[CONFLICT]** — the February reporting (Legal Era) describes the matter as adjourned and **pending**; the March digest reports it as **refused**. The reconciliation is almost certainly that it was adjourned in February and dismissed in March, but **I could not obtain the order itself and could not pin the exact date of the refusal.**
- Sources: [Legal Era, 10 February 2026](https://www.legaleraonline.com/from-the-courts/supreme-court-examines-constitutional-challenge-to-income-tax-act-2025-provisions-980052); [LiveLawBiz — PIL filing](https://www.livelawbiz.com/income-tax/supreme-court/pil-in-supreme-court-challenges-income-tax-law-allowing-search-of-digital-devices-cloud-data-522655); [LiveLawBiz Direct Tax Monthly Digest: March 2026](https://www.livelawbiz.com/digests/monthly-digests/livelawbiz-direct-tax-monthly-digest-march-2026-528491); [Bar & Bench column, "When the taxman comes for your cloud"](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/when-the-taxman-comes-for-your-cloud-the-pil-challenging-indias-digital-search-powers); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/supreme-court-refuses-plea-over-invasive-seizure-powers-for-tax-officials/)

Several Indian government primary-source domains (**indiacode.nic.in**, **pib.gov.in**, **incometaxindia.gov.in** PDFs, **bjp.org**) blocked automated fetching with HTTP 403 during this research. The Law Commission's 2018 paper is a scanned image PDF. Items 14, 19, 20 and the BJP manifesto gap all stem from this and would be quickly resolved by a human opening those pages in a browser.
