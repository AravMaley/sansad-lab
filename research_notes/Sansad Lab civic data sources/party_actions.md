# Party Actions Layer — "What they actually did"

**Purpose.** This file is a SEPARATE evidence layer for statements where a party's **2024 Lok Sabha
manifesto is silent**. It records demonstrated positions from actions, official statements, Parliament
answers and votes.

**Hard rule for the site: NEVER merge this layer with the manifesto-coded layer.** Manifesto silence
stays coded as silent. This layer renders only as a clearly labelled "What they actually did" panel.

**Scope:** 2019 – October 2026. **Compiled:** 27 October 2026.

## Evidence-type legend

Every item is tagged with one of:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `GOVT` | Official Government of India position — Parliament answer, PIB release, Cabinet decision, court affidavit |
| `PARTY` | Official party position — resolution, manifesto of a state unit, formal party statement |
| `LEADER` | An individual leader's remark; indicative, not binding |
| `STATE` | A state-government scheme or act by a party's state government, not a national commitment |
| `VOTE` | A recorded vote or floor position in Parliament |

**Important caveat for the whole file:** a `GOVT` position is the position of the Union Government
(an NDA coalition), which is not identical to the BJP as a party. A `STATE` scheme is the act of one
state government and does not establish a national party position. The site copy should say this.

## 1. MSP guaranteed by law

**Demonstrated position: has NOT accepted a legal/statutory MSP guarantee. Repeatedly offers
administrative alternatives (annual MSP fixation, expanded procurement, a review committee) instead.
Confidence: HIGH.**

### Strongest primary source — Lok Sabha answer, 28 July 2026 `GOVT`

- **Who:** Shri Ramnath Thakur, Minister of State for Agriculture and Farmers Welfare.
- **When:** Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 1598, answered 28 July 2026. Asked by Shri Rajabhau Parag
  Prakash Waje.
- **What:** Part (c) of the question asked directly "whether the Government proposes to enact a legal
  framework guaranteeing remunerative prices for agricultural produce, **including a statutory
  guarantee for MSP**, if not, the reasons therefor".
  The answer does **not** say yes. It answers entirely by listing existing administrative measures:
  > "The Government has taken various measures to provide remunerative prices to the farmers for their
  > agricultural produce. Minimum Support Price (MSP) for 22 notified agricultural crops are fixed
  > annually by the Government. Since 2014, farmers have received more than Rs. 27.80 lakh crore through
  > MSP procurement of about 12819 LMT of agricultural produce."

  It then describes FCI/CCI/JCI procurement, PM-AASHA, the Price Support Scheme, Price Deficit Payment
  Scheme, Market Intervention Scheme, the new Price Differential Payment component (2024-25) and the
  Price Stabilisation Fund. It also restates that the MSP policy "ensures a minimum margin of 50 percent
  over the all-India weighted average cost of production".
- **Analytical note:** this is a *non-denial non-commitment* — the question asked "if not, the reasons
  therefor" and the reply gives neither a yes nor explicit reasons. Code it as "has not committed",
  not as "has ruled out".
- **Source:** https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU1598_LJJBy5.pdf

### The committee route — Sanjay Agrawal MSP Committee, July 2022 `GOVT`

- **Who:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare; chaired by **Sanjay Agrawal**, former Agriculture
  Secretary.
- **When:** Constituted by notification in **July 2022**, ~8 months after the farm laws repeal.
  *Date discrepancy:* one Rajya Sabha answer cites S.O. 3211(E) dated **12 July 2022**; another cites
  notification 3053 dated **18 July 2022**; press reports say 18 July. **Flag as unresolved.**
- **What:** ~26 members. Terms of reference: make MSP "more effective and transparent", increase the
  autonomy of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), promote natural/zero-budget
  farming and crop diversification. **Note: the ToR do not include examining a legal guarantee.**
- **Samyukta Kisan Morcha refused to nominate members**, rejecting the committee's mandate.
- **Progress:** as of a **7 February 2025** Rajya Sabha answer, 6 main meetings + 39 sub-committee
  meetings. A later written reply by Shivraj Singh Chouhan put the total at **45 meetings**. **No final
  report had been submitted** at the time of the most recent reply located.
- **Sources:**

    sansad.in Rajya Sabha question archive*)
  - https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/740348/1/PQ_259_10022023_U993_p217_p220.pdf
    (RS PQ 259, 10 Feb 2023 — cites S.O. 3211(E) of 12 July 2022)
  - https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/news/india/modi-govt-sets-up-msp-panel-8-months-after-withdrawing-farm-laws
  - https://sabrangindia.in/article/no-msp-committee-until-centre-clarifies-mandate-skm (SKM rejection)

### Response to the 2024 farmer protests — the 5-year contract offer `GOVT`

- **Who:** Union Ministers **Piyush Goyal, Arjun Munda and Nityanand Rai**.
- **When:** **18 February 2024**, fourth round of talks with farmer unions, Chandigarh.
- **What:** Instead of a statutory guarantee, the Centre offered a **five-year contract** under which
  government agencies (NCCF, NAFED, Cotton Corporation of India) would buy **pulses (arhar, urad,
  masur), maize and cotton** at MSP, with "no limit on the quantity". Goyal called the talks "positive".
- **Outcome:** On **19 February 2024** farmer leader **Jagjit Singh Dallewal** said the proposal was
  "not in the interest of farmers" and it was rejected; the 'Dilli Chalo' march resumed 21 February.
  Sarwan Singh Pandher demanded a **central ordinance** on a legal MSP guarantee instead.
- **Significance:** the offer is itself evidence of the government's preferred instrument —
  **contract-based procurement, not statute.**
- **Sources:**

## 2. A nationwide caste census — **CLEAR REVERSAL**

**Demonstrated position BEFORE 2025: against caste enumeration in the Census, described as a
"conscious policy decision". AFTER 30 April 2025: in favour — caste enumeration to be included in
Census 2027. Confidence: HIGH.**

### BEFORE — position 1: Lok Sabha, 20 July 2021 `GOVT`

- **Who:** **Nityanand Rai**, Minister of State for Home Affairs (written reply).
- **When:** **20 July 2021** (a Tuesday). *Note: The Wire dates it 25 July; 20 July is corroborated by
  Deccan Herald, India TV and Tribune and is almost certainly correct.*
- **What:** The Government "has decided as a matter of policy not to enumerate caste-wise population
  other than SCs and STs in Census". A later reply added that no caste-wise enumeration other than
  SC/ST has been done in any Census since Independence.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.deccanherald.com/national/no-caste-census-since-independence-centre-tells-lok-sabha-1079448.html
  - https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/no-plan-to-enumerate-caste-data-in-census-2021-other-than-scs-sts-govt-223260
  - https://www.aninews.in/news/national/general-news/no-caste-based-census-other-than-scs-sts-since-independence-mos-nityanand-rai20211130160333/
  - https://thewire.in/government/barring-the-enumeration-of-scs-sts-no-caste-census-since-independence-centre (date disputed)

### BEFORE — position 2: affidavit in the Supreme Court, September 2021 `GOVT`

- **Who:** Union of India / Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment, in *State of Maharashtra* v.
  *Union of India* (OBC data case).
- **When:** Affidavit filed **September 2021**; Supreme Court dismissed Maharashtra's plea
  **15 December 2021** (Khanwilkar & C.T. Ravikumar JJ).
- **What:** The Centre told the Court that a caste census of Backward Classes is
  **"administratively difficult and cumbersome"**, and that excluding such information from the Census
  is a **"conscious policy decision"**. It said SECC 2011 caste data was **unusable** for official
  purposes (enumerator errors, entries containing numbers and symbols) and that, unlike SC/ST data,
  there is no constitutional obligation to collect OBC Census figures.
  Maharashtra's counsel Shekhar Naphade countered that the government had earlier told a Parliamentary
  committee the data was **98.87% error-free**.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.thequint.com/news/politics/caste-census-maharashtra-government-centre-supreme-court-backward-classes-difficult-cumbersome
  - https://www.verdictum.in/top-stories/supreme-court-dismisses-maharashtras-plea-to-direct-centre-to-disclose-secc-2011-raw-caste-data-of-obcs-1343960
  - https://www.deccanherald.com/india/supreme-court-dismisses-maharashtras-plea-for-use-of-socio-economic-caste-census-2011-data-1061118.html
  - https://www.newsclick.in/SC-rejects-Maharashtra-plea-direct-Centre-disclose-data-OBCs-per-2011-caste-census

### AFTER — the reversal: 30 April 2025 `GOVT`

- **Who announced it:** **Ashwini Vaishnaw**, Union Minister for Information & Broadcasting, at a
  post-Cabinet media briefing.
- **Deciding body:** the **Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs (CCPA)**.
- **When:** **30 April 2025**.
- **What:** Caste enumeration will be **included in the forthcoming (national) Census**, rather than
  conducted via separate state surveys. Vaishnaw's stated rationale: some states had conducted caste
  surveys "non-transparently", creating doubt, and the Census is the Centre's domain. He also attacked
  the Congress/INDIA bloc for using the caste census as a "political tool" and cited Manmohan Singh's
  2010 Lok Sabha assurance.
- **Significance:** this is the **clearest documented policy reversal** in this whole file. Present
  before/after side by side with both dates.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/caste-census-to-be-conducted-alongwith-national-census-union-minister-ashwini-vaishnaw-makes-big-announcement-attacks-congress-india-bloc-474223-2025-04-30
  - https://www.aninews.in/news/national/politics/caste-enumeration-to-be-part-of-forthcoming-population-census-says-government20250430165114/
  - https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/union-cabinet-approves-caste-enumeration-in-upcoming-census
  - https://www.deccanherald.com/india/modi-govt-decides-to-includes-caste-enumeration-in-upcoming-census-3518289

### AFTER — follow-through and the gazette controversy, 16 June 2025 `GOVT`

- **When:** Gazette notification by the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, **16 June 2025**.
- **What:** Declares "a census of the population of India shall be taken during the year 2027", with a

"change if required" and the services have proposed raising retention. No notified policy change found

## 4. Direct annual cash transfer to women

**Demonstrated position: BJP state governments have adopted monthly/annual DBT cash transfers to women
as a signature instrument. NO national/central scheme of this kind was found. Confidence: HIGH on the
state record; MEDIUM-HIGH on "no national scheme" (absence of evidence — see Gaps).**

### STATE — Madhya Pradesh: Mukhyamantri Ladli Behna Yojana `STATE`

- **Who:** then CM **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** (BJP).
- **When:** announced **28 January 2023**; formally launched **5 March 2023**; first instalment
  **June 2023** (one source gives 10 June 2023 as "launch" — flag minor discrepancy).
- **What:** ₹1,000/month to eligible women, later raised to **₹1,250**, and to **₹1,500** in
  **November 2025**.
- **Later strain:** CM **Mohan Yadav** described the scheme as a **"heavy burden"** on the exchequer
  (report dated 6 October 2026) — useful, non-partisan evidence of fiscal cost.
- Sources:

### STATE — Maharashtra: Mukhyamantri Majhi Ladki Bahin Yojana `STATE`

- **When:** announced in the **state budget, 28 June 2024**; benefits backdated to July 2024; publicly
  launched around **17 August 2024** (Raksha Bandhan). *Sources conflict on the formal launch date —
  flag.*
- **What:** **₹1,500/month** DBT to eligible women. Enrolment extended to 30 September 2024. Widely

### STATE — Odisha: Subhadra Yojana `STATE`

- **Who launched:** **Prime Minister Narendra Modi**, in Bhubaneswar, under CM **Mohan Charan Majhi**

## 5. Full statehood for Jammu & Kashmir

**Demonstrated position: committed in principle to restoring statehood, but only "at an appropriate
time", with no date given; not restored as of October 2026. Confidence: HIGH.**

## B. Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 `VOTE` — **FAILED**

**The government (BJP/NDA) moved it; the INC opposed it and it was defeated. First failure of a
constitutional amendment bill under the Modi government.**

- **Introduced:** Lok Sabha, **16 April 2026**, by **Arjun Ram Meghwal**, Union Minister of Law &
  Justice, alongside the **Delimitation Bill, 2026** and the **Union Territories Laws (Amendment)
  Bill, 2026**.
- **Voted:** **17 April 2026**. **298 for, 230 against**, out of **528 present and voting**. A two-thirds
  majority (352) was required; the government fell **54 votes short**. PRS records the status as
  **"Negatived"**.
- **What it would have done:**
  1. Raised the maximum Lok Sabha strength from **550 to 850** (up to 815 from states, 35 from UTs).
  2. Amended **Article 82** to remove the requirement that delimitation wait for the first Census after
     2026 — letting Parliament decide, by ordinary law, the timing and the census used.
  3. Amended **Article 334A** so the one-third women's reservation applies **immediately after
     delimitation**, rather than after a post-2026-census exercise.
  - The companion Delimitation Bill would have used the **2011 Census**.
- **PRS-flagged consequences:** Tamil Nadu 39 → 32 seats; Uttar Pradesh 80 → 89 (at current House
  strength); the Lok Sabha:Rajya Sabha ratio moving from ~2.2:1 to ~3.3:1; the Council of Ministers cap
  rising from ~81 to ~122; and "Delimitation Commission" left undefined in the Bill.
- **INC position — opposed:**
  - **Rahul Gandhi** (Leader of the Opposition), 16-17 April 2026: *"This is not a Women's Bill. This
    has nothing to do with the empowerment of women."* and *"We will defeat this Bill."*
    **(Source: Congress's own published debate synopsis — a party source. Corroborate against the Lok
    Sabha transcript or a news report before publishing the quote.)**
  - **Gaurav Gogoi**, opening for the Congress: supports women's reservation but wants it applied to the
    **current 543-seat House**, implemented on passage; *"Do not link it with delimitation"*; called the
    Bill a **"backdoor attempt"** at delimitation and questioned the 850 figure in Clause 3.
  - **K.C. Venugopal**, 18 April 2026 (after the vote): *"Their agenda to link delimitation with
    women's reservation failed."* He asked where the promised "50 per cent" guarantee was and said a
    formal government amendment would have been the proper route.
- **Other opposition:** TMC's **Abhishek Banerjee** said 2011-Census-based expansion raised "serious
  concerns about fairness and balance". The broad opposition objection was that it would
  **disproportionately reduce southern and north-eastern representation**.
- **Aftermath:** **Kiren Rijiju** withdrew the Delimitation Bill, 2026 and the Union Territories Laws
  (Amendment) Bill, 2026. PRS marks the Delimitation Bill **"infructuous"** as of 17 April 2026.
- **Consequence noted by analysts:** with delimitation now dependent on the 2027 Census, women's
  reservation is unlikely to apply to the **2029** Lok Sabha election.
- **Not established:** no source located gives a **party-wise division list**. Do **not** assert "all
  INC MPs voted against" — say the INC opposed the Bill and it was defeated 298-230.

## C. Farm Laws Repeal Bill, 2021 `VOTE`

**Both parties were for repeal. The dispute was over the absence of debate and over a legal MSP.**

- **Lok Sabha, 29 November 2021:** introduced ~12:09 pm, passed by **voice vote** by ~12:13 pm,
  **with no debate**, amid opposition sloganeering in the well. Speaker **Om Birla** said discussion
  would happen only if members returned to their seats; when they did not, he put the Bill to a voice
  vote.
- **Rajya Sabha, same day:** passed after a **brief discussion**.
- **Government rationale — Agriculture Minister Narendra Singh Tomar:** both the government and the
  opposition favour repeal, "so there is no need for any discussion on the repeal Bill."
- **INC position:** supported repeal but demanded a debate. **Adhir Ranjan Chowdhury** (Leader of the
  Congress in the Lok Sabha) said the party wanted a discussion on the three laws **and a law on MSP**.
- **Other reactions:** Omar Abdullah — "passed without discussion; repealed without discussion";
  Supriya Sule called it "a black day for democracy". Farmer leader Rakesh Tikait said protesters would
  not leave before MSP was discussed.
- **Links to item 1:** the demand for a statutory MSP was raised at the moment of repeal and has not
  been conceded — the MSP committee (July 2022) was the government's answer.
