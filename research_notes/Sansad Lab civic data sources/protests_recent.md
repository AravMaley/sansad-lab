## 1.1 The "cockroach" remark — origin of the name

### Takeaway
On **15 May 2026** Chief Justice of India **Surya Kant**, hearing a contempt matter connected to fake law degrees and senior-advocate designations, made an oral observation reported as likening unemployed youngsters to "cockroaches" and "parasites". He issued a clarification the next day saying he had meant people practising on fraudulent degrees, not the country's youth. The clarification did not catch up with the insult: by 16 May a satirical party had taken the word as its name.

### Cited findings
- Al Jazeera's quotation of the remark: *"There are youngsters like cockroaches, who don't get any employment or have any place in the profession."* He is also reported in the same hearing to have said "parasites" were attacking the system, and to have described some people who become media figures, social-media voices or activists as attacking everyone — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)
- Clarification: the remark was "for those with fake degrees" — [The Quint, 17 May 2026](https://www.thequint.com/news/breaking-news/cockroach-comment-for-those-with-fake-degrees-clarifies-chief-justice-of-india-surya-kant)
- In a later interview the CJI said the observation arose during a discussion of lawyers who entered the profession on fake or unrecognised law degrees, and used the episode to call for a **protocol on how oral court observations are reported** — [Bar & Bench, 24 July 2026](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/exclusive-cji-surya-kant-clarifies-cockroach-remark-calls-for-protocol-on-reporting-oral-court-observations)
- Critical commentary, for balance: [The Wire, "CJI's 'Cockroaches' Remark Does Not Bode Well for Democracy"](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/cjis-cockroaches-remark-does-not-bode-well-for-democracy)
- Context: the remark landed during a week of nationwide student protests over paper leaks that had already forced the cancellation of a medical entrance test — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)

### Attribution note (non-partisan framing)
Two framings coexist and the site should carry both: (a) the CJI says he was misquoted and was describing degree fraud; (b) critics, including The Wire and the movement itself, say the words were used of unemployed young people and of activists, and that the clarification came only after backlash. Do not assert either as settled.

## 1.2 How it started, who leads it, how it organises

### Takeaway
The CJP is a Gen Z, internet-native movement founded by one person in 24 hours with AI tools, which went from a joke post to street protests in three weeks. It is **not a registered political party** with the Election Commission. It built a formal office-bearer structure only in August 2026, after it had already won its first concession.

### Cited findings
- **Founder:** **Abhijeet Dipke**, a political-communications strategist, roughly 30-31 years old, a public-relations graduate of Boston University; he was in Chicago when he launched it and gave his first interviews from there — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest); [Reuters profile, 23 July 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/abhijeet-dipke-face-indias-first-gen-z-protest-movement-2026-07-23/)
- **Founded 16 May 2026**, the day after the CJI's remark. Dipke posted on X asking what would happen if all the cockroaches came together, then stood up a website, logo, manifesto and social accounts within 24 hours, saying he used Claude and ChatGPT for the design and the manifesto text — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)
- **The name is a deliberate parody of the Bharatiya Janata Party** — same cadence, "Cockroach" substituted for "Bharatiya" — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest); [Boom Live explainer, 18 May 2026](https://www.boomlive.in/explainers/the-cockroach-janta-party-how-a-cji-comment-became-a-movement-31586)
- **Scale of following:** 11.1 million Instagram followers and 350,000+ sign-ups via a Google Form within the first days ([Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)); reported as **15-20 million** within the first week (Wikipedia, citing a headline figure of 20 million; [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/cockroach-janta-party-protest-was-swarming-with-aspirants-job-seekers-but-not-enough-women/2952609/) also gives "20 million+"). **Treat as a range: ~11m (mid-May, Al Jazeera) rising to a claimed 15-20m.**
- **Joining criteria (satirical, and widely quoted):** be unemployed, lazy, chronically online, and able to rant professionally. Slogan: **"Voice of the Lazy & Unemployed."** — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)
- **Leadership formalised 6 August 2026**, when a National Working Committee of 12 office-bearers was announced and Dipke was elected **National Convenor**: **Saurav Das** (Co-Convener and Chief Spokesperson), **Ashutosh Ranka** (Co-Convener, Lead Policy Strategist), **Aafreen Nawaz** (National Secretary), **Ajinkya Shinde** (National Organisation In-charge), with **Ratna Singh** as legal head — [Indian Express, 6 Aug 2026](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/pune/cockroach-janata-party-cjp-national-working-committee-abhijeet-dipke-10821186/)
- **Not registered with the ECI** (per Wikipedia's infobox); at least three trademark applications have been filed over the name. *Flagged: single-source.*
- **Campus wing — Cockroach Students' Union of India (CSUI):** presented as the CJP's **student wing**, created on or about **20 May 2026**, four days after the party. Its members took part in the 6 June Jantar Mantar protest. **This is weakly sourced**: the Wikipedia article on CSUI is tagged for missing citations and possible deletion, and no mainstream outlet confirming its founding date was found. Use cautiously, or describe it simply as "a student wing that emerged within days."
- **Allied organisations on the ground** (distinct from CJP, and important for accuracy): the **All India Students' Association (AISA)**, whose national president **Neha Bora** features repeatedly, and the **Students' Federation of India (SFI)**, both affiliated to Left parties — [Al Jazeera, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/indias-cockroach-leaders-detained-new-delhi-in-lockdown)
- **Political sympathisers** named early on: MPs Mahua Moitra and Kirti Azad, retired bureaucrat Ashish Joshi, lawyer Prashant Bhushan — [Al Jazeera, 20 May 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/5/20/cockroach-janata-party-top-indian-judges-comment-sparks-satire-protest)

## 1.3 The exams

### Takeaway
The hard, documented triggers are two: the **NEET-UG 2026 paper leak** and the **CBSE on-screen marking (OSM) irregularity**. **SSC** appears as a grievance voiced by protesters on the ground rather than as a named leak. **UGC-NET** could not be verified as a 2026 trigger and should not be listed.

### Cited findings
- **NEET-UG 2026** was held on **3 May 2026** with around **2 million candidates**; the National Testing Agency **cancelled it on 12 May 2026** over leak allegations and referred the matter to the CBI; a re-test followed in June — Wikipedia (2026 Delhi Jantar Mantar protests); corroborated on scale and cancellation by [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/india/cockroach-revolt-testing-times)
- **CBSE On-Screen Marking irregularity** — discrepancies in results — cited alongside NEET as a founding grievance — Wikipedia; [ThePrint, 6 June 2026](https://theprint.in/india/cockroach-janta-party-protest-was-swarming-with-aspirants-job-seekers-but-not-enough-women/2952609/)
- **SSC (Staff Selection Commission):** protesters interviewed at Jantar Mantar on 6 June included SSC aspirants describing repeated delays and leaks — one said she had sat the SSC exam several times without success "mainly because of delays or paper leaks" — [ThePrint, 6 June 2026](https://theprint.in/india/cockroach-janta-party-protest-was-swarming-with-aspirants-job-seekers-but-not-enough-women/2952609/)
- **UGC-NET: NOT VERIFIED for 2026.** UGC-NET appears only in the context of the separate 2024 NEET/UGC-NET controversy and a Congress protest. Do not put it on the timeline.

## 1.6 Police action, injuries, arrests, deaths

### Takeaway
**No protester deaths are reported from police action in phase one.** The deaths attached to this movement are the **suicides of NEET aspirants** after the leak, which formed the basis of the compensation demand. Injury figures on 20 July are genuinely disputed: the police count protester injuries at 60, the CJP at ~150, hospital sources at 100+.

### Cited findings — the 20 July crackdown
- Police used **tear gas, canes and baton charges**; metro station exits near Jantar Mantar were sealed, so protesters alighted earlier and walked. Delhi Police denied that a scuffle occurred; circulating video showed riot police using canes to push people back — [Al Jazeera, 20 July 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/20/police-attack-cockroach-activists-as-thousands-march-on-indian-parliament)
- **Delhi Police figures: nearly 180 injured — 60 protesters and 118 security/police personnel — and 70 protesters detained**, with legal action to follow — [Al Jazeera, 21 July 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/21/indias-cockroach-protest-movement-halts-marches-after-police-violence)
- **CJP figure: ~150 protesters wounded** (Dipke), later put as "about 150 hospitalised" and "more than 100 arrested" — [Al Jazeera, 21 July 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/21/indias-cockroach-protest-movement-halts-marches-after-police-violence); Wikipedia
- **Hospital sources: 100+ protesters injured**; Delhi Police also reported **15-20 government vehicles damaged** — Wikipedia (2026 Delhi Jantar Mantar protests)
- **Pellet guns:** a hospital report confirmed one protester had pellet wounds; **police denied using pellet guns**. A video of a DCP slapping a woman protester circulated widely — Wikipedia. *Report as a contested allegation.*
- **Amnesty International** said the police action showed how peaceful dissent is being suppressed in India — [Al Jazeera, 21 July 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/21/indias-cockroach-protest-movement-halts-marches-after-police-violence)

- **Supreme Court-appointed panel:** a Supreme Court panel probing the July violence at Jantar Mantar launched a public complaints portal, prioritising the alleged use of pellet guns and the harassment of female protesters during the 20 July march — [The Hindu, 17 Oct 2026](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/supreme-court-appointed-panel-launches-portal-for-complaints-over-july-violence-at-jantar-mantar/article71596471.ece)

## 1.8 Where it stands in October 2026

The education phase closed on 25 July. The movement then pivoted to the Election Commission (Section 2) and has been met with a far harder state response: **Jantar Mantar was denotified as Delhi's designated protest site on 11 October 2026**, and CJP leaders were detained three times in October.

## 2.1 Gyanesh Kumar's appointment and the appointment law

### Takeaway
Gyanesh Kumar was appointed CEC in **February 2025** by a three-member panel of the PM, the Home Minister and the Leader of the Opposition — a composition created by a **2023 Act** that replaced the Supreme Court's own 2023 prescription of PM + LoP + CJI. Rahul Gandhi filed a dissent note. PILs against the 2023 Act were pending in the Supreme Court on the day Kumar took office.

### Cited findings
- Appointed February 2025, succeeding Rajiv Kumar, who retired on 18 February 2025. The selection committee was chaired by PM Modi, with Rahul Gandhi as LoP and Home Minister Amit Shah as the PM's nominee — [Vajiram & Ravi current affairs](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/appointment-of-gyanesh-kumar-as-chief-election-commissioner/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/gyanesh-kumar-assumes-charge-as-cec)
- **The law:** the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, enacted after the Supreme Court's *Anoop Baranwal* ruling, **left the CJI out** of the panel. Critics say this gives the executive a 2-1 majority — [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/appointment-of-gyanesh-kumar-as-chief-election-commissioner/)
- Rahul Gandhi submitted a **dissent note** and asked for deferral while the Supreme Court considered the matter, calling the late-night decision "disrespectful" and "discourteous" — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/disrespectful-for-pm-to-have-made-midnight-decision-to-appoint-new-cec-rahul/amp)
- The government's position: it acted under the law as it stands — [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/appointment-of-gyanesh-kumar-as-chief-election-commissioner/)
- On the day Kumar took charge, the Supreme Court **deferred** PILs challenging the 2023 Act to March; ADR was among the petitioners — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/supreme-court-defers-hearing-on-pils-challenging-election-commissioners-appointment)
- **April 2026:** the Rajya Sabha Chairman (C.P. Radhakrishnan) and Lok Sabha Speaker (Om Birla) **rejected** the Opposition's notice of a motion for Kumar's removal — [News on AIR, 6 April 2026](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/rs-chairman-cp-radhakrishnan-and-ls-speaker-om-bilra-reject-impeachment-notice-of-motion-against-cec-gyanesh-kumar). A CEC can be removed only in the manner of a Supreme Court judge (Article 324(5)), so this was the constitutional route and it was closed off at the admission stage.

## 2.2 The Special Intensive Revision (SIR)

### Takeaway
The SIR is a door-to-door re-verification of electoral rolls requiring documentary proof, with citizenship checks for anyone not on a "legacy" roll. It ran in Bihar in 2025, then nationally in three phases. Across the exercise roughly **130 million names** have been dropped from draft or final rolls. The ECI says this removes the dead, the migrated and duplicates; the Opposition says it is targeted disenfranchisement.

### Cited findings — Bihar (Phase I, 2025)
- **24 June 2025:** ECI orders the Bihar SIR, ~4 months before the state election. Aadhaar, EPIC, ration cards and NREGS documents were **not** on the accepted list — [Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/politics/revising-electoral-rolls-disenfranchise-millions-threaten-citizenship)
- **1 August 2025 draft roll:** ~**72.4 million** names, about **6.5 million fewer** than before. ECI attribution: **2.2m dead, 3.6m migrated, 0.7m duplicates**. A Hindustan Times analysis found no discernible pattern in the deletions — [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3wnx7689l5o)
- ADR's reading of the Bihar CEO's own records gave a narrower breakdown and found about **9,968 removals with no stated reason** — [ADR](https://adrindia.org/content/bihar-ceo-records-reveal-unexplained-deletions)
- **14 August 2025: the Supreme Court directed the ECI to publish a booth-wise list of electors left out of the draft roll**, with a compliance report by 22 August. The ECI posted the lists on district magistrates' websites; the CEC said this was done within 56 hours — [Supreme Court Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/reports/revision-of-electoral-rolls-in-bihar-day-5-sc-directs-eci-to-publish-information-of-deleted-names/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/names-of-65-lakh-voters-deleted-from-bihar-sir-posted-on-district-websites); [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/ec-bihar-deleted-voters-supreme-court-order)
- **Final Bihar roll, 30 September 2025: 74.2 million voters.** Net removal over the exercise ~**47 lakh (4.7m), about 5.95%**, down from 7.89 crore to 7.42 crore
- *Note the two circulating figures — "65 lakh" (draft-stage exclusions) and "47 lakh" (final net removal). They measure different things; the site should not treat them as rival estimates of the same quantity.*

### Cited findings — Phase II (announced 27 October 2025)
- 12 states and UTs (one Wikipedia passage says "nine States and three Union Territories", which is the same set counted differently): Andaman & Nicobar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Puducherry, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal. **Assam excluded** pending the NRC
- **West Bengal, 28 February 2026:** ~**6.18 million** names deleted and a further ~6 million "under adjudication"; Malda and Murshidabad worst affected — [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/how-west-bengal-sir-different-from-other-states-10564447/)
- **11-13 April 2026, Phase II totals:** electorate down from **50.99 crore to 45.81 crore, −10.2%**. Uttar Pradesh lost ~**2.04-2.05 crore** names; West Bengal ~**83.86 lakh** (first reported as 90.8 lakh) — [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/consolidated-voters-list-of-9-states-and-3-uts-that-underwent-sir-phase-ii-trimmed-by-102/article70853877.ece)
- **13 April 2026:** the Supreme Court flagged the margin of error in the Bengal deletions but **refused to intervene**; deleted voters could appeal to special tribunals, and the Assembly election went ahead without them — [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/supreme-court-flags-margin-of-error-in-bengal-sir-refuses-to-intervene/articleshow/130231882.cms)

### The objections, stated fairly
- **Opposition / petitioners:** the document list excludes the IDs poor and migrant voters actually hold; the timelines are too short; the citizenship check converts the ECI into a citizenship tribunal; deletions correlate with electoral outcomes. Prannoy Roy's post-Bengal analysis found a correlation between deletions and the swing against the TMC, and that Form 7 (exclusion) requests succeeded **97.5%** of the time against ~**50%** for Form 6 (inclusion) — [DeKoder analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K5qUfsPBSvQ). *This is a commentator's analysis, not an official finding.*
- **ECI:** the exercise is a routine statutory revision under s.21(3) RP Act 1950 read with Article 324; citizenship verification is "only for electoral purposes" and the method is "liberal, soft-touch" — [LiveLaw, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/sir-verification-of-citizenship-only-for-electoral-purposes-the-method-is-liberal-soft-touch-eci-tells-supreme-court-519854). The ECI also argues that none of the deleted Bihar voters came to court or filed objections, and that Congress's own booth-level agents in Haryana filed no objections.
- **BJP:** alleges that large numbers of undocumented Bangladeshi migrants fraudulently entered the rolls, which the SIR removes — [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/asia-pacific/20261018-india-s-cockroach-movement-founder-released-from-police-custody)
- **Internal dissent (the September 2026 bombshell):** Election Commissioners **Sukhbir Singh Sandhu** and **Vivek Joshi** objected **on record at least 14 times over 10 months** to SIR decisions they said were taken without their knowledge — [Indian Express, 25 Sep 2026](https://indianexpress.com/article/express-exclusive/election-commission-sir-14-objections-gyanesh-kumar-sukhbir-singh-sandhu-vivek-joshi-10889737/). **The ECI disputes this**, saying its decisions including those on SIR had the approval of all three commissioners.

## 2.6 Where it stands, 27 October 2026

- **Gyanesh Kumar has not resigned and remains CEC.** No report of resignation was found.
- The **removal motion was rejected at the admission stage** in April 2026 by the Rajya Sabha Chairman and Lok Sabha Speaker.
- The **Supreme Court upheld the SIR in May 2026** and has refused interim relief; the fresh Opposition petition is at the affidavit stage.
- **SIR Phase III continues**, with Karnataka's final roll due today and Maharashtra's on 4 November.
- **Protest itself has been constrained**: Jantar Mantar denotified, repeated detentions, a one-year peace bond on the CJP's three senior leaders, and an undertaking not to call protests at the site.

- **Net: nothing the protesters demanded has been conceded.** The one tangible gain in 15 months is the August 2025 court-ordered disclosure of deleted names.

## 3.2 Jamia Millia Islamia and AMU, 15 December 2019

### Takeaway
Police entered both campuses on the same evening. At Jamia they went into the library. This is the single most consequential day of the movement: it converted a regional protest into a national one, and it is the day Shaheen Bagh began.

### Cited findings
- **Jamia:** police entered the campus, used batons and tear gas, **more than 200 students injured**, about **100 detained overnight** — Wikipedia (Citizenship Amendment Act protests)
- **AMU, Aligarh:** police entered the campus that evening and attacked students; **at least 80 injured** — Wikipedia
- **The library:** a set of photographs taken at Jamia's reading hall, uploaded to Commons under **CC BY 4.0**, documents scattered chairs and books, students' abandoned belongings, blood stains on a chair, and tear-gas residue — primary visual evidence, see 3.6
- **Student accounts vs police accounts diverge sharply** and should both be reported: students and the university said police entered without permission and beat students who were studying; Delhi Police maintained they were pursuing rioters who had mingled with the crowd after buses were burned nearby. *This specific police justification was not re-verified in this round of searching — see Gaps.*

## 3.3 Shaheen Bagh

### Takeaway
A sit-in by Muslim women, many of them elderly, blocking a stretch of road in south-east Delhi for **101 days**. It became the symbol of the movement and spawned imitations across India. It ended not by negotiation or concession but by police clearance under the COVID-19 lockdown.

### Cited findings
- **15 December 2019 to 24 March 2020 — 101 days** — Wikipedia (Shaheen Bagh protest), dates corroborated by [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2020/03/24/delhi-police-clear-shaheen-bagh-some-protesters-detained.html) and [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/3/24/shaheen-bagh-anti-caa-sit-in-removed-amid-coronavirus-lockdown) for the clearance
- Led and sustained **by women**, many elderly; Reuters described it as **the longest-running protest against the citizenship law** — [Gulf News/Reuters](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fasia%2Findia%2Findia-police-clear-out-shaheen-bagh-protests-citing-coronavirus-1.70588548)
- **10 January 2020:** the Delhi High Court dismissed a plea to remove the protesters — Wikipedia
- **How it ended:** on **24 March 2020**, the day after the national lockdown was announced, Delhi Police cleared the site. Police asked protesters to leave; **at least nine were detained, six of them women**; television showed bulldozers taking down tents and billboards. About 50 people were present — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2020/03/24/delhi-police-clear-shaheen-bagh-some-protesters-detained.html); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/3/24/shaheen-bagh-anti-caa-sit-in-removed-amid-coronavirus-lockdown)
- **It never resumed.** On **7 October 2020** the Supreme Court held in *Amit Sahni v. Commissioner of Police* that indefinite occupation of public spaces is not permissible — Wikipedia. **This is the clearest legal legacy of the protests, and it cuts against protesters: the tactic itself was curtailed.**
- **Imitators:** Park Circus (Kolkata), Agripada (Mumbai), Bilal Bagh (Bengaluru) — [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/books/how-the-caa-protests-ignited-hope-and-prompted-indians-to-reimagine-citizenship)

- **Trigger, contested:** Al Jazeera reports the violence erupted after a mob led by a governing-party leader targeted anti-CAA sit-ins. The BJP and Delhi Police dispute this account and have pursued a conspiracy case against anti-CAA organisers. **Both framings must be given.**
- **Prosecutions:** activists **Umar Khalid** and **Sharjeel Imam** were charged under the UAPA in the "larger conspiracy" case and spent years in custody; the Supreme Court was still deferring their bail pleas in 2025 — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/2020-delhi-riots-sc-defers-hearing-on-bail-pleas-of-umar-khalid-sharjeel-imam-to-october-31-3776362)
- **Acquittal rate:** speakers at a sixth-anniversary press event in 2026 said that of **116 adjudicated cases, about 93 ended in acquittal**, including murder cases. **This is a claim made at a press event, not an official court tally** — [OB News](https://www.obnews.co/Flow/News/id/14024035.html)
- **An important framing caution:** the Delhi riots were communal violence in which both communities were attacked and both were accused, not simply "a crackdown on protesters". The site should treat them as a consequence of, and connected to, the protest period — not as part of the protests themselves.

### C. Arrests and injuries overall
- **3,000+ arrests** nationally by 17 December 2019; **175 injuries** as of 16 December; **~1,200 detained in Delhi on 19 December**; **357 detained in Delhi on 27 December** — Wikipedia infobox and body. *These are Wikipedia aggregates; the underlying news citations were not individually fetched.*

## 3.5 Scale, and what changed

### Scale
- **94 of India's 732 districts, across 14 of 29 states**, within three weeks of the Act's passage — a figure that circulates widely; traced here to an academic/aggregator source, **not** a primary count ([Bharatpedia mirror](https://en.bharatpedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests))
- **"Hundreds of thousands" nationally** — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2020/12/18/one-year-mass-caa-protests-india-muslims-citizenship-amendment-act-modi/)
- Single-city estimates, **all contested and none from a primary source**: Mumbai ~1.5 lakh; Hyderabad "Million March" 100,000+; Kochi anywhere from 50,000 to 500,000. **Give these as ranges with the caveat, or omit.**
- Commentators called it the most significant upheaval since the **Emergency of 1975-77** — [Radical Philosophy](https://www.radicalphilosophy.com/commentary/nationalisms-by-against-and-beyond-the-indian-state)

### What changed — the honest ledger
**Not won:**
- **The Act was not repealed, amended or stayed.** It came into force 10 January 2020; the **Rules were notified on 11 March 2024**; the Supreme Court has repeatedly declined to stay it, including on 19 March 2024 — [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/reports/citizenship-amendment-act-supreme-court-refuses-to-stay-the-caa-rules-directs-union-to-file-responses-to-interim-stay-applications/)
- **237 petitions remain pending** in the Supreme Court, the oldest filed in December 2019. **Over five years without a merits hearing** — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/more-than-230-petitions-against-caa-pending-in-supreme-court-since-2019-599524)
- No independent judicial inquiry into the UP police violence; **no FIRs against police in Meerut** a year later — [Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/crime/meerut-five-muslim-caa-protests-shot-police-families-allege)
- Yogi Adityanath did not resign and was re-elected in 2022
- The Supreme Court **restricted the protest tactic itself** in October 2020

**Partially won / changed:**
- **Six assemblies passed resolutions against the Act**: Kerala (31 Dec 2019), Punjab (17 Jan 2020), Rajasthan (25 Jan 2020), West Bengal (27 Jan 2020), Puducherry (~12 Feb 2020, first UT), Telangana (16 Mar 2020). Several states additionally announced they would not implement the NRC or the NPR. These resolutions have **no legal force** — citizenship is a Union List subject — but they were a real federal statement
- **Kerala invoked Article 131** to sue the Union directly, the first state to do so (14 Jan 2020)
- **The nationwide NRC has not been carried out.** *Causation unproven — COVID-19, cost and politics all intervened — so do not claim the protests stopped it.*

**The honest summary for the site:** the largest protest movement in India since the Emergency did not change a single word of the law it opposed. Its effects were federal, cultural and electoral rather than legislative.

## 3.6 Vivid, verifiable detail (pick one)

- **101 days, and a reading room for children.** Shaheen Bagh ran a library and art corner for children on the road, alongside the sit-in; photographs of it are on Commons, CC0 — [File:The reading and art section for kids at Shaheen Bagh 15 Jan 2020.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_reading_and_art_section_for_kids_at_Shaheen_Bagh_15_Jan_2020.jpg)
- **The photo wall.** Jamia students curated a wall of photographs at Shaheen Bagh on 28 January 2020, turning the protest into its own archive — several Commons images document it
- **Blood on a library chair.** The Commons photo set of Jamia's reading hall, taken on 2 January 2020 and released CC BY 4.0, includes images captioned as blood stains on a chair, students' abandoned belongings, and tear-gas remains
- **They had to put it in writing to the courts.** The UP government told the Allahabad High Court that 22 people had been killed, while the Chief Minister told the Assembly that no one died from police bullets — the two statements sit side by side in the record
