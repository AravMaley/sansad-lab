## 1. The Chipko Movement

**Dates:** First action 24 April 1973 (Mandal, Chamoli district); best-known action 26 March 1974 (Reni village); the campaign's main legislative win came in 1980. Treat as **1973–1981** for the timeline, with a long afterlife.

**Where:** Chamoli and Tehri Garhwal districts of the Garhwal Himalaya, then in Uttar Pradesh, now Uttarakhand.

**Trigger (plain):** The state forest department refused a local labour co-operative, the Dasholi Gram Swarajya Sangh, its annual request for ten ash trees to make farm tools — then awarded 300 ash trees in the same forest to Simon Company, a sporting-goods maker in Allahabad. Villagers who had just watched floods and landslides tear through deforested slopes decided to physically stop the loggers. — [Wikipedia: Chipko movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)

**Who led it / how many:**
- Chandi Prasad Bhatt (Dasholi Gram Swarajya Sangh) and Sunderlal Bahuguna were the two best-known organisers; the actual tree-hugging was overwhelmingly done by village women. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)
- Mandal, 24 April 1973: **about a hundred** villagers and DGSS workers confronted the lumbermen. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)
- Reni, March 1974: **27 women** led by **Gaura Devi** confronted contractors sent to fell around 2,500 trees, in a four-day stand-off. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)
- No reliable total participant count exists; this was a dispersed village movement, not a single rally.

**Demanded:** An end to commercial clear-felling by outside contractors in the Himalayan catchments; local community rights over forest produce; recognition that forests are watershed and livelihood, not timber stock.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- The Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister, Hemwati Nandan Bahuguna, appointed a committee after Reni; it **ruled in the villagers' favour**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)
- In **1980 Prime Minister Indira Gandhi ordered a fifteen-year ban on commercial green felling** in the Himalayan forests of Uttar Pradesh, reportedly after meeting Sunderlal Bahuguna. — [Wikipedia: Chipko movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement); [Wikipedia: Sunderlal Bahuguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderlal_Bahuguna); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/chipko-an-unfinished-mission-30883)
  - **Disputed:** Earth.org calls it a **ten-year** ban. Most sources say fifteen. — [Earth.org](https://earth.org/50-years-on-the-legacy-of-the-chipko-movement/)
- Measurable effect: recorded output of major forest produce from eight hill districts fell from over 62,000 cubic metres in 1971 to 40,000 in 1981. — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/chipko-an-unfinished-mission-30883)
- Felling restrictions were later extended to Himachal Pradesh and Karnataka. — [Green Network Asia](https://greennetwork.asia/gna-knowledge-hub/chipko-movement-the-anti-deforestation-tree-hugging-protest-by-rural-women-in-india/)
- **Be honest:** local forest rights were *not* restored. Chipko stopped the contractors but left the forest department in charge; Down To Earth's retrospective is titled "Chipko: an unfinished mission."

**Parliament / legislation link:** The **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** — which requires central government approval before forest land is diverted to non-forest use — was passed in the same year and in the same political climate, and is widely credited to the pressure Chipko generated. Treat the causal link as *credited by commentators*, not proven: secondary summaries assert it, and I did not find a parliamentary debate record tying the Act to Chipko. — [PolSci Institute](https://polsci.institute/globalisation-environment-development/chipko-movement-eco-feminism-forest-conservation/)

**Deaths / violence:** None recorded. Chipko was conspicuously non-violent — which is part of why it travelled so well internationally.

**Vivid detail:** At Reni on the day of the felling, the contractors' men were lured away to Chamoli on the pretext of collecting compensation cheques. Gaura Devi, left in the village with the women, led 27 of them into the forest and they kept an all-night vigil with their arms around the trees. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipko_movement)

**Note a discrepancy for fact-checkers:** Wikipedia's narrative section dates the Reni logging day to **25 March 1974** while its Legacy section says the Chipko action was **26 March 1974**. Use "late March 1974" or flag it.

## 2. The Emergency and the Resistance to It (JP Movement)

**Dates:** Bihar Movement from 18 March 1974; JP's "Sampoorna Kranti" call 5 June 1974; **Emergency proclaimed late on 25 June 1975**; Emergency ended **21 March 1977**; election held 16–20 March 1977.

**Where:** Nationwide. Began in Gujarat (Navnirman) and Bihar (Patna); the Emergency itself was national; Delhi, Turkman Gate and the Delhi demolitions are its most-photographed sites.

**Trigger (plain):** Student agitations against corruption, inflation and unemployment in Gujarat and Bihar grew into a national opposition movement under Jayaprakash Narayan. On 12 June 1975 the Allahabad High Court found Indira Gandhi guilty of corrupt electoral practices, unseated her and barred her from office for six years; thirteen days later, facing JP's call for the police and army to refuse illegal orders, she had the President proclaim a national Emergency. — [Wikipedia: The Emergency (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))

**Key dates:**
- **18 March 1974** — Bihar Movement begins as a student protest against corruption in the state government. — [Wikipedia: Bihar Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_Movement)
- **5 June 1974** — JP gives the call for **Sampoorna Kranti (Total Revolution)** before a mass gathering at Gandhi Maidan, Patna. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/50-years-of-jps-call-for-sampoorna-kranti-tributes-paid-in-patna/2118867/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/bihar/50-years-of-jayaprakash-narayans-call-for-sampoorna-kranti-tributes-paid-in-patna-3055202)
- **12 June 1975** — Justice Jagmohanlal Sinha of the Allahabad High Court unseats Indira Gandhi. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **24 June 1975** — Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer grants a conditional stay, letting her stay PM pending appeal. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **25 June 1975, just before midnight** — President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed proclaims Emergency under **Article 352** on "internal disturbance" grounds. The Cabinet was told afterwards and ratified it the next morning. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **18 January 1977** — elections called; **16–20 March 1977** — polling; **21 March 1977** — Emergency revoked. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))

**Who led it / how many:** Jayaprakash Narayan ("Loknayak"), with Morarji Desai, Charan Singh, Atal Bihari Vajpayee, L.K. Advani, George Fernandes, Raj Narain and the Jana Sangh/RSS, socialist and Lok Dal networks. The resistance was mostly underground after June 1975 — the mass expression came at the ballot box in March 1977, when roughly 194 million voters were enrolled.

**Demanded:** Initially the resignation of corrupt state governments; after June 1975, the lifting of the Emergency, release of detainees, restoration of press freedom and fresh elections.

**WHAT CHANGED — this is the big one:**
- **The government fell.** The Janata alliance and partners won **298 seats** (plus allies, giving a two-thirds supermajority); Congress fell to **154**, down 198. Indira Gandhi lost her own Rae Bareli seat to Raj Narain by more than 55,000 votes; Sanjay Gandhi lost Amethi. Morarji Desai became India's first non-Congress Prime Minister. — [Wikipedia: The Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India)); [Wikipedia: 1977 Indian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Indian_general_election)
- **The Constitution was changed twice, in opposite directions.** The **38th Amendment** (1975) barred judicial review of the Emergency proclamation and actions under it; the **42nd Amendment** (1976) massively expanded executive power at the judiciary's expense. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- The **44th Amendment Act, 1978**, passed by the Janata government, reversed much of this: it replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" as a ground for a national Emergency, required the Cabinet's **written** advice, required parliamentary approval within one month, and barred suspension of Articles 20 and 21 even during an Emergency. *(This is the single cleanest "protest → constitutional amendment" arc on the site's theme; the 44th Amendment is not covered on the Emergency Wikipedia page I fetched — source it from the Constitution text or PRS before publishing.)*
- The **Shah Commission** of Inquiry (set up May 1977, chaired by former Chief Justice J.C. Shah) documented the abuses in three volumes, 525 pages. — [Wikipedia: Shah Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Commission)
- **Be honest:** the Janata government collapsed within three years and Indira Gandhi returned to power in 1980. The durable win was the 44th Amendment and the fact that no national Emergency has been declared since.

**Parliament / legislation link:** Maximal. Parliament's term was extended during the Emergency; the 38th, 39th and 42nd Amendments were passed by a Parliament with the opposition in jail; the 44th Amendment undid them. MISA (the Maintenance of Internal Security Act, 1971) was the detention statute and was repealed in 1978.

**Deaths / detentions / violence (figures disputed — give the range):**
- **34,988** detained under MISA and **75,818** under the Defence and Internal Security of India Rules; **total 110,806** — the Shah Commission's own figures, as reported in the Wikipedia infobox and by the Supreme Court Observer. The Wikipedia article notes these totals included ordinary criminals. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India)); [Supreme Court Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/journal/the-constitutional-sanction-for-preventive-detention-reeks-of-a-fear-of-freedom/)
- **Amnesty International estimated 140,000** detained without trial over about twenty months — a higher figure than the Commission's. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- PIB (Government of India) puts the MISA figure at "nearly 35,000." — [PIB factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=149224&reg=3&lang=1)
- **Forced sterilisation: 8.3 million sterilisations in 1976–77**, up from 2.7 million the previous year, most of them coerced. Sanjay Gandhi's personal responsibility is disputed between historians. One Haryana village, Uttawar, saw 800 forced sterilisations. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **Muzaffarnagar, 18 October 1976:** police fired on a crowd resisting the sterilisation drive; **30 died**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **Demolitions:** 150,105 structures demolished in Delhi during the Emergency (41,252 in 1975; 99,073 in 1976; 7,641 up to 23 March 1977); about **700,000 people displaced** in Delhi alone. Turkman Gate is the best-known site; the Wikipedia article I fetched gives **no death toll** for Turkman Gate — do not publish a number without a primary source. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- **P. Rajan**, a Kerala engineering student, was arrested on 1 March 1976 and died in police custody; his body was never recovered. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))

**Vivid detail (pick one):**
- Electricity to the major Delhi newspaper offices was cut within three hours of the proclamation. The *Times of India* (Bombay) then ran a mock obituary: **"Democracy, beloved husband of Truth, loving father of Liberty, brother of Faith, Hope and Justice, expired on June 26."** The *Indian Express* ran a blank editorial on 28 June; the *Financial Express* printed Tagore's "Where the mind is without fear" in large type. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))
- Alternative, for the Parliament theme: Subramanian Swamy was expelled from Parliament on 10 August 1976 after rising on a point of order to say that "Democracy had also died." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_(India))

## 3. The Assam Movement (Assam Andolan)

**Dates:** 8 June 1979 – 15 August 1985.

**Where:** Assam, centred on Guwahati; violence concentrated in Nagaon, Darrang, Goalpara and Kamrup districts.

**Trigger (plain):** Preparing for a by-election in the Mangaldoi Lok Sabha seat in 1979, officials found roughly 47,000 doubtful names on the draft roll; of about 36,000 cases processed, some 26,000 — over 72% — were confirmed as non-citizens. The All Assam Students' Union concluded that migration from Bangladesh had swamped the electoral rolls and demanded that "foreigners" be detected, struck off the rolls and deported. — [Wikipedia: Assam movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)

**Who led it / how many:**
- The **All Assam Students' Union (AASU)** and the **All Assam Gana Sangram Parishad (AAGSP**, formed 26 August 1979). Public faces: **Prafulla Kumar Mahanta** (AASU president) and **Bhrigu Kumar Phukan** (general secretary). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)
- Scale: in a November 1979 satyagraha an estimated **700,000 people courted arrest in Guwahati and about 2 million statewide**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)
- The movement started with a 12-hour AASU bandh on **8 June 1979**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)

**Demanded:** Detection, disenfranchisement and deportation of post-1951 (later, post-1971) migrants; revision of the electoral rolls before any election; constitutional safeguards for Assamese language, land and political representation.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- **The Assam Accord, signed 15 August 1985** between AASU/AAGSP and the Government of India, ended the movement. It set **24 March 1971** as the cut-off date for citizenship in Assam, and Clause 6 promised constitutional, legislative and administrative safeguards for "the Assamese people." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement) *(Wikipedia's text mentions the Accord and Clause 6 but does not state the 15 August 1985 signing date; cite the Accord text at mha.gov.in alongside.)*
- **Section 6A of the Citizenship Act, 1955** was inserted by Parliament in **1985** to give the Accord legal effect: people of Indian origin who entered Assam before 1 January 1966 were deemed citizens; those who entered between 1 January 1966 and 24/25 March 1971 could register as citizens after a ten-year wait. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-upholds-validity-of-s6a-of-citizenship-act-recognizing-assam-accord-by-41-majority-272689)
- **The protesters took power.** AASU leaders formed the Asom Gana Parishad and won the December 1985 state election; Prafulla Mahanta became Chief Minister at 33.
- **Supreme Court, 17 October 2024:** a five-judge Constitution Bench **upheld Section 6A by 4:1**. CJI D.Y. Chandrachud with Justices Surya Kant, M.M. Sundresh and Manoj Misra in the majority; Justice J.B. Pardiwala dissenting, holding it unconstitutional with prospective effect. Justice Kant: fraternity "cannot be understood in a narrow manner to hold that one should be able to choose one's neighbours." — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/supreme-court-upholds-validity-of-s6a-of-citizenship-act-recognizing-assam-accord-by-41-majority-272689); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/10/17/supreme-court-upholds-validity-section-6a-citizenship-act-landmark-4-1-ruling/)
- **Be honest:** forty years on, the Accord's core promise is unfulfilled. The Illegal Migrants (Determination by Tribunals) Act, 1983 made it "practically impossible" to prove someone was an illegal alien (struck down by the Supreme Court in 2005). Assam's NRC, updated under Supreme Court supervision, produced a final draft in 2019 which the state government has since **refused to accept as a legal document**. Clause 6 safeguards remain largely unimplemented. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)

**Parliament / legislation link:** Very strong. Section 6A of the Citizenship Act (1985), the IMDT Act 1983, the NRC, and the direct line from the Assam Accord to the 2019 Citizenship (Amendment) Act protests (see §10) and to the 2025–26 SIR (see §11) — the Assam Movement is where "revising the electoral roll to find non-citizens" enters Indian politics.

**Deaths / violence (disputed — state the range):**
- **The Nellie massacre, 18 February 1983.** A mob, mainly Tiwa and lower-caste Hindu, killed Bengali-origin Muslims across 14 villages in Nagaon district in roughly six hours. Death tolls:
  - **1,819** — the figure in official reports, per Newsclick. — [Newsclick](https://www.newsclick.in/nellie-1983-revisited-victims-say-they-had-been-barricaded-6-months-massacre)
  - **2,191** — "the official estimate," per Genocide Watch. — [Genocide Watch](https://www.genocidewatch.com/single-post/forgetting-nellie-forty-two-years-and-counting)
  - **2,000–3,000** — Wikipedia's range. — [Wikipedia: Nellie massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellie_massacre)
  - **up to 10,000** — unofficial estimates. — [Genocide Watch](https://www.genocidewatch.com/single-post/forgetting-nellie-forty-two-years-and-counting)
  - **Use a range and name the claimants.** No one was ever prosecuted; the Tewary Commission report was not made public for decades.
- **About 3,000 killed** during the February 1983 election violence overall (Baruah 1986, cited in Wikipedia). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)
- The Swahid Smarak Kshetra memorial at Boragaon commemorates **860** agitators who died over the course of the movement. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)
- The state deployed about 150,000 armed personnel for the boycotted February 1983 election. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)

**Vivid detail:** The movement's first martyr was **Khargeswar Talukdar**, a 22-year-old AASU worker beaten to death at Bhabanipur in December 1979 — less than six months after the first bandh. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_movement)

## 4. Narmada Bachao Andolan (Save the Narmada Movement)

**Dates:** 1985 – **ongoing** (the Sardar Sarovar dam was inaugurated in 2017; rehabilitation litigation and protest continue).

**Where:** The Narmada valley across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat; focal villages Manibeli, Domkhedi, Jalsindhi, Badwani, Nandurbar. Campaigning also in Delhi and Washington DC.

**Trigger (plain):** The Narmada Water Disputes Tribunal's 1979 award cleared a cascade of dams on the Narmada, including raising the Sardar Sarovar. Villagers — many of them Adivasi, most without formal land titles — found they would be submerged with no serious plan to resettle them. — [Wikipedia: Narmada Bachao Andolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)

**Key dates:**
- **6 October 1969** — Narmada Water Disputes Tribunal constituted. **12 December 1979** — its award released, approving 30 major, 135 medium and 3,000 small dams. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)
- **1985** — Medha Patkar founds the NBA. **1989** — Patkar and others testify in Washington DC; Baba Amte publishes *Cry O Beloved Narmada*. **1991** — the independent **Morse Commission** is set up; Patkar and Amte win the Right Livelihood Award. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)
- **June 1992** — the Morse report finds the Indian government and the World Bank both responsible for the project's failures, condemning the absent resettlement plan and inadequate environmental assessment. — [Bretton Woods Project](https://www.brettonwoodsproject.org/2017/10/controversial-sardar-sarovar-project-dam-set-to-displace-thousands/); [World Bank timeline](https://timeline.worldbank.org/en/timeline/eventdetail/1911)
- **31 March 1993** — the **$450m World Bank loan is cancelled**; India asked for the cancellation shortly before the Bank would have withdrawn. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan); [World Bank timeline](https://timeline.worldbank.org/en/timeline/eventdetail/1911)
- **18 October 2000** — Supreme Court judgment in *Narmada Bachao Andolan v. Union of India*, W.P. (C) 319 of 1994, AIR 2000 SC 3751. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1938608/)
- **1999–2006** — construction resumes and is declared complete. **2017** — Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurates the dam. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)

**Who led it / how many:** **Medha Patkar** (founder and the movement's face), **Baba Amte**; Arundhati Roy as supporter and publicist; Alok Agarwal and local leaders in the valley. Participation was in the low tens of thousands across hundreds of villages, mobilised through long marches (the 1990 Jan Vikas Sangharsh Yatra) and village-level satyagrahas — not single mass rallies.

**Demanded:** A halt to construction until a full review of costs, benefits and displacement; land-for-land resettlement before submergence; and later, that dam height not rise ahead of rehabilitation.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- **The World Bank withdrew** from Sardar Sarovar in 1993 after the Morse review — the first time an independent review had forced the Bank out of a major project. This reshaped World Bank resettlement and environmental policy globally and led to the creation of its Inspection Panel. — [World Bank timeline](https://timeline.worldbank.org/en/timeline/eventdetail/1911); [Bretton Woods Project](https://www.brettonwoodsproject.org/2017/10/controversial-sardar-sarovar-project-dam-set-to-displace-thousands/)
- **Supreme Court, 18 October 2000 (2:1):** the Court **allowed construction to continue**, upholding the Tribunal award, but tied dam height to rehabilitation — no further raising until oustees were properly resettled — and required monitoring by **Grievance Redressal Authorities** in each state. **Justice Ruma Pal dissented**, arguing construction should stop until rehabilitation concerns were resolved. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1938608/); [The Lawscape](https://www.thelawscape.in/narmada-bachao-andolan-v-union-of-india-2000-development-vs-environment/)
- **Be honest: the dam got built.** The NBA did not stop Sardar Sarovar. Its enduring achievements are procedural — the principle that resettlement must precede submergence, the Grievance Redressal Authorities, the global shift in development-bank practice, and the vocabulary of "development-induced displacement" in Indian public life.

**Parliament / legislation link:** Weaker than the others, and worth saying so. The decisive arenas were the Tribunal, the World Bank and the Supreme Court, not Parliament. The indirect legislative legacy is in the law on displacement and land acquisition — the **Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013** carries the argument the NBA made for three decades. *(Treat the LARR link as thematic, not sourced to a parliamentary record — verify before asserting causation.)*

**Displacement figures (badly disputed — give the range and the claimant):**
- **40,000 families** in Madhya Pradesh still unrehabilitated at the 2017 inauguration — **Medha Patkar's claim**, reported by Bretton Woods Project. — [Bretton Woods Project](https://www.brettonwoodsproject.org/2017/10/controversial-sardar-sarovar-project-dam-set-to-displace-thousands/)
- **at least 100,000 people** displaced on completion — Harvard Human Rights Journal estimate. — [HHRJ](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/hrj/wp-content/uploads/sites/83/2020/06/19HHRJ275-Ramachandra.pdf)
- Patkar in a later interview: 50,000 families resettled across three states, 20,000 given land or cash, about 15,000 still in limbo. — [Counterview](https://www.counterview.net/2022/05/world-bank-proved-right-narmada-is.html)
- Official project authority figures are lower. **Do not publish a single number.** Say: "estimates range from about 40,000 families still unresettled (NBA) to 100,000+ people displaced overall (academic estimates); official figures are lower, and the count turns on whether 'major sons', landless labourers and undocumented families are included."

**Deaths / violence:** No mass casualties. The NBA office was attacked in 1994 and Patkar and other activists were assaulted, arrested and force-fed during hunger strikes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)

**Vivid detail:** The movement's signature tactic was ***jal samarpan*** — standing in the rising waters of the monsoon reservoir and refusing to move, daring the state to let them drown. Activists held these satyagrahas at Manibeli from 1991 onward, and Commons holds a 2017 photo from a Narmada children's camp at Manibeli. *(The Wikipedia article I fetched does not describe jal samarpan or Manibeli; source this from NBA's own archive at narmadaandolan.org or contemporaneous Frontline/EPW reporting before publishing.)*

**Counter-view, for non-partisanship:** Critics argue the NBA delayed a project that now supplies drinking water and irrigation to drought-prone Gujarat and Rajasthan, that some supporters intimidated families who accepted compensation, and that the Supreme Court has at times considered perjury allegations over land-ownership claims. Present both sides. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_Bachao_Andolan)

## 5. The Anti-Mandal Commission Protests

**Dates:** August 1990 – late 1990 (acute phase); the legal arc closes with *Indra Sawhney*, **16 November 1992**.

**Where:** Delhi (Delhi University, especially Deshbandhu College), and across north and central India — UP, Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan — plus Hyderabad. Largely absent in the South where OBC reservation already existed and in the East.

**Trigger (plain):** On 7 August 1990, Prime Minister V.P. Singh told the Lok Sabha that his government would implement the Mandal Commission's recommendation of **27% reservation in central government jobs and public-sector undertakings for Other Backward Classes**, taking total reservation to 49.5%. Upper-caste students, who saw their chances in a shrinking public sector collapse, erupted. — [The Polis Project](https://thepolisproject.com/read/the-bumpy-road-of-reservations-the-7-august-1990-announcement-on-the-mandal-commissions-recommendations-by-v-p-singh/); [Wikipedia: Mandal Commission protests of 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990)

**Key dates:**
- **1 January 1979** — the Mandal Commission (Second Backward Classes Commission, chaired by **B.P. Mandal**, then MP for Madhepura) is appointed under Article 340 by the **Morarji Desai** Janata government; its first part (Volumes I–II) was released **31 December 1980** and then shelved for a decade. It identified 3,743 castes as socially and educationally backward. — [Wikipedia: Mandal Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission); [Legacy IAS](https://www.legacyias.com/mandal-commission-background-recommendations/)
  - **Date discrepancies to flag:** some sources give the appointment as **20 December 1978** (1 January 1979 is probably the formal notification), and the Wikipedia protests article wrongly says "January 1977" — Desai did not become PM until March 1977. The report date is given as both 30 and 31 December 1980.
- **7 August 1990** — V.P. Singh's announcement in the Lok Sabha. — [The Polis Project](https://thepolisproject.com/read/the-bumpy-road-of-reservations-the-7-august-1990-announcement-on-the-mandal-commissions-recommendations-by-v-p-singh/)
- **13 August 1990** — **Office Memorandum No. 36012/31/90-Estt(SCT)** issued, giving the decision effect; modified by a further OM of **25 September 1990**. The OMs were reproduced in the *Indra Sawhney* judgment, which records that they "were not in fact acted upon on account of the orders made by this Court." — [CLPR copy of *Indra Sawhney*](https://clpr.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/09._Indra_Sawhney_Ors._v_UOI.pdf)
- **19 September 1990** — **Rajiv Goswami**, a student at Deshbandhu College, Delhi University, sets himself on fire. — [Logically Facts](https://www.logicallyfacts.com/en/fact-check/image-from-1990-falsely-passed-as-hindu-attack-on-sikh-man) *(Wikipedia's article says "October 1990" — the 19 September date is the one most widely reported; flag the discrepancy.)*
- **7 November 1990** — V.P. Singh loses a confidence vote **356 to 151** and resigns. — [Wikipedia: Premiership of V.P. Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiership_of_V._P._Singh)
- **16 November 1992** — *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India*, nine-judge bench, 6:3. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra_Sawhney_and_Others_v._Union_of_India)

**Who led it / how many:** Largely leaderless and student-driven — college unions, coaching-centre students and traders' associations in north Indian towns. Tactics were bandhs, hartals, dharnas, rail and road blockades, and self-immolation. The counter-mobilisation in support of Mandal was led by OBC politicians and intellectuals; the BJP, which withdrew support from Singh over L.K. Advani's arrest during the Rath Yatra on 23 October 1990, is often conflated with the anti-Mandal camp — **keep the two causes distinct in the copy.** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990)

**Demanded:** Withdrawal of the 27% OBC quota; "merit, not caste" in public appointments; in some versions, economic rather than caste criteria for reservation.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- **The government fell** on 7 November 1990 — though the immediate cause was the BJP's withdrawal over the Rath Yatra, not Mandal alone. Be precise about this.
- **The Supreme Court stayed implementation** in October 1990 and then, in ***Indra Sawhney* (16 November 1992)**, a nine-judge bench **upheld the 27% OBC quota 6:3**, with three qualifications that still structure Indian reservation law:
  1. total reservation should ordinarily not exceed **50%**;
  2. the **"creamy layer"** of OBCs must be excluded;
  3. no reservation in promotions (later reversed by Parliament).
  Lead opinion by Justice B.P. Jeevan Reddy; bench led by CJI M.H. Kania. — [Wikipedia: Indra Sawhney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra_Sawhney_and_Others_v._Union_of_India); [CLPR judgment PDF](https://clpr.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/09._Indra_Sawhney_Ors._v_UOI.pdf)
- **The protesters lost; the reservation stands.** 27% OBC reservation in central employment has been in force since 1993 and was extended to central higher education by the 93rd Amendment (2005) and the Central Educational Institutions (Reservation in Admission) Act, 2006.
- **Parliament then overruled parts of the judgment.** The 77th (1995), 81st (2000), 82nd (2000) and 85th (2001) Amendments restored reservation in promotions and carry-forward of backlog vacancies, each enacted to undo a specific *Indra Sawhney* holding. In 2019 the **103rd Amendment** added a 10% EWS quota, breaching the 50% ceiling — upheld by the Supreme Court in *Janhit Abhiyan* (2022). *(Amendment list is from standard constitutional history; verify each against the Constitution text / PRS before publishing.)*

**Parliament / legislation link:** Excellent, and unusually two-directional: a Prime Minister announces a policy in the Lok Sabha, the street reacts, the Court sets limits, and Parliament then amends the Constitution repeatedly to work around the Court. This is the best entry on the timeline for showing Parliament *responding to* a judgment.

**Deaths (disputed — give both):**
- **Ramachandra Guha: about 200 attempted self-immolations, more than 60 deaths.** — cited in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990)
- **159 attempts, 63 deaths** — the other figure in circulation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990); also [Dibakar Dutta explainer](https://dibakardutta.in/anti-mandal-commission-obc-reservation-student-protests-explained/) (a blog, uncited — do not use alone)
- Wikipedia names individual victims, which humanises the entry: **Surinder Singh Chauhan** (22, New Delhi, described as the first fatality), **Monica Chadha** (19, New Delhi), **Anil Kumar** (15, Tekri, Haryana), **Saurabh Verma** (20, Indore), **Susarla Vamsee Mohan** (20, Hyderabad), **Narinder Kaur** (16, Pathankot), **Saroj Saini** (15, Chandigarh). Note how many were teenagers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990)
- **Recommended wording:** "Estimates of deaths range from about 60 to more than 60 out of roughly 160–200 self-immolation attempts; the historian Ramachandra Guha gives the higher attempt figure. No official count was ever published."

**Vivid detail:** Rajiv Goswami **survived** the self-immolation that made him the face of the agitation, with roughly 70% burns. He was later elected president of the Delhi University Students' Union — and died in 2004 of complications from those burns, fourteen years after the fire. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission_protests_of_1990); [Logically Facts](https://www.logicallyfacts.com/en/fact-check/image-from-1990-falsely-passed-as-hindu-attack-on-sikh-man)

## 6. The Right to Information Campaign (MKSS)

*Included because it is the clearest "protest → statute" arc in modern India, and because it is a rural, non-metropolitan movement that balances the list.*

**Dates:** c. 1990 (MKSS registered) – **12 October 2005** (RTI Act in force). Peak: the 40-day Beawar dharna, April–May 1996.

**Where:** Central Rajasthan — Devdungri, Bhim, Beawar (Ajmer district) — then nationwide through the NCPRI.

**Trigger (plain):** Landless labourers and marginal farmers in Rajasthan who were being paid below the minimum wage on government works discovered they could not challenge the muster rolls because they were not allowed to see them. The demand for wages became a demand for the records. — [Wikipedia: Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdoor_Kisan_Shakti_Sangathan); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/10/04/rti-movement-india-history-beawar-devdungari-village.html)

**Who led it / how many:** The **Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)** — Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey, Shankar Singh and local leaders including Sushila and Lal Singh — scaling up into the **National Campaign for People's Right to Information (NCPRI)**. Participants were in the thousands, drawn from across rural Rajasthan; **rural women formed the largest contingent** at Beawar. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/10/04/rti-movement-india-history-beawar-devdungari-village.html)

**Demanded:** The legal right to inspect and copy government records — muster rolls, bills, vouchers — and the right to hold *jan sunwais* (public hearings) where officials answer to villagers.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- A **September 1995 mass meeting at Beawar** is described as the first public demand in India for a right to information in law; about six months later, in **April 1996**, MKSS began a **40-day dharna at Chang Gate, Beawar** (some sources say 44 days). — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/10/04/rti-movement-india-history-beawar-devdungari-village.html); [School for Democracy RTI Museum](https://schoolfordemocracy.org/rti-museum/)
- Rajasthan passed a state right-to-information law; nine states had such laws before the central Act.
- **The Right to Information Act, 2005:** passed by the **Lok Sabha on 11 May 2005**, the **Rajya Sabha on 12 May 2005**, presidential assent **15 June 2005**, gazetted 21 June 2005, **fully in force 12 October 2005**. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/know-the-law/right-to-information-act-2005-all-you-need-to-know-159485); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/right-to-information-act-2005-a-comprehensive-overview/)
- **Be honest about the present:** the RTI Act has been substantially weakened since — the RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019 gave the central government control over Information Commissioners' tenure and salaries, and Section 44(3) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 amended Section 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act to bar disclosure of all "personal information." *(Both amendments are well documented; verify dates against PRS before publishing.)*

**Parliament / legislation link:** The strongest on the list — a village sit-in over muster rolls that ends in a statute every Indian can use.

**Deaths / violence:** No deaths during the campaign itself. Since 2005, dozens of RTI users have been killed; the Commonwealth Human Rights Initiative maintains the tally. Worth one line, with a current figure sourced at build time.

**Vivid detail:** The movement's slogan — painted on banners at Beawar and still used — was ***"Hamara paisa, hamara hisaab"***: our money, our accounts. The oddity that struck observers was that the rural poor were on hunger strike demanding not food or land but **photocopies of government records**. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/theweek/cover/2025/10/04/rti-movement-india-history-beawar-devdungari-village.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazdoor_Kisan_Shakti_Sangathan)

**Images:** Not checked in detail. Commons holds `File:A.P.J. Abdul Kalam and eminent Social Activist Shri Anna Hazare at the inaugural the National Convention on 'One Year of RTI' … October 13, 2006.jpg` (a PIB release — verify GODL/CC terms). Search Commons for "Aruna Roy" and "Nikhil Dey" — both have portraits on Commons, licences unverified. **Flagged as an image gap.**

## 7. The Telangana Statehood Movement (2009–14 phase)

**Dates:** 29 November 2009 – 2 June 2014. (The movement's roots run back to the 1969 Jai Telangana agitation; the timeline entry should cover the decisive 2009–14 phase.)

**Where:** The ten districts of Telangana within Andhra Pradesh, centred on Hyderabad — Osmania University, Tank Bund, Necklace Road — plus Warangal, Karimnagar and the Singareni coalfields.

**Trigger (plain):** On 29 November 2009, K. Chandrashekar Rao of the Telangana Rashtra Samithi began a fast unto death demanding that the Congress government introduce a Telangana statehood bill in Parliament. His deteriorating health, and the general strikes that followed, forced the Centre's hand within eleven days. — [Wikipedia: Telangana movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_movement)

**Key dates:**
- **29 Nov 2009** — KCR begins his fast. **6–7 Dec** — general strikes shut Telangana. **7 Dec** — all-party meeting backs a separate state.
- **9 Dec 2009** — Home Minister **P. Chidambaram announces** the process of forming Telangana will begin. Jairam Ramesh later said KCR's failing health was a major factor.
- **23 Dec 2009** — **the Centre reverses itself**, saying nothing will happen until all parties agree. Protests in Andhra and Rayalaseema (the Samaikyandhra counter-movement) had erupted; MLAs from those regions resigned.
- **3 Feb 2010** — five-member **Srikrishna Committee** appointed; **6 Jan 2011** — its 505-page report released.
- **17 Feb 2011** — 16-day non-cooperation movement, **about 300,000 government employees** participating.
- **10 Mar 2011** — the **Million March** at Tank Bund, Hyderabad: **about 50,000 reached the venue**; 16 statues on Tank Bund were damaged.
- **13 Sep – 24 Oct 2011** — ***Sakala Janula Samme*** ("the strike of all people"), a 42-day general strike including **about 60,000 Singareni coal miners**; a rail blockade on 15 Oct cancelled 110 trains and thousands were arrested, including 8 MPs and 4 MLAs.
- **30 Sep 2012** — the **Telangana March** on Necklace Road, **about 200,000** people; police vehicles torched.
- **30 Jul 2013** — Congress Working Committee resolves to create Telangana. **3 Oct 2013** — Union Cabinet approves.
- **13 Feb 2014** — the bill is introduced in the **Lok Sabha**; Seemandhra MP **Lagadapati Rajagopal uses pepper spray in the House**, members are evacuated and some hospitalised.
- **18 Feb 2014** — **Lok Sabha passes the bill by voice vote**, with the live broadcast blacked out during the vote (widely criticised). **20 Feb 2014** — **Rajya Sabha passes it**. **1 Mar 2014** — presidential assent and gazette publication.
- **2 Jun 2014** — **Telangana is formed**, India's 29th state, with Hyderabad as shared capital for ten years; KCR becomes first Chief Minister.
— all of the above from [Wikipedia: Telangana movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_movement)

**Who led it / how many:** K. Chandrashekar Rao and the TRS; the **Telangana Joint Action Committee (TJAC)** under Prof. M. Kodandaram, which pulled in student unions (Osmania University was the engine), employees' associations, lawyers, teachers and the Singareni miners' unions. Peak single-event turnout: TRS claimed **2.6 million** at a Warangal public meeting on 16 December 2010 — an organiser's figure, treat with caution. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_movement)

**Demanded:** A separate state of Telangana carved out of Andhra Pradesh, on grounds of unfulfilled 1956 safeguards, under-investment, and capture of Hyderabad's growth by coastal Andhra elites.

**WHAT CHANGED:** **They got the state.** The **Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014** passed both Houses and Telangana came into existence on 2 June 2014 — the clearest, most complete win of any movement on this timeline. The TRS went on to win the 2014 and 2018 state elections. *(Note the honest coda: the TRS/BRS lost power to the Congress in December 2023, and several Accord-era promises on Hyderabad revenue-sharing and job quotas remain contested.)*

**Parliament / legislation link:** Direct and dramatic — a protest movement whose demand is literally a bill, passed in a House filled with pepper spray and with the cameras switched off. Article 3 of the Constitution (Parliament's power to form new states) is the hook.

**Deaths (badly disputed — handle carefully):**
- **Srikanth Chary**, a young man in Hyderabad, set himself on fire on **29 November 2009** after KCR's arrest and died of his burns on **3 December 2009**. He is Telangana's officially commemorated "statehood martyr." — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/telangana-remembers-statehood-martyr-srikanth-chary); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/featured/260523/remembering-ts-statehood-heroes.html)
- **20 July 2011** — Yadi Reddy, a 30-year-old driver, was found dead near Parliament House in Delhi; his note said he was distressed that no state had been created. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_movement)
- **Totals are contested and politically charged.** Figures found: **885** deaths between 29 Nov 2009 and 9 Dec 2012 (from a book, *Telangana Movement Suicides, Sacrifices, Martyrs*, cited second-hand); **"over 600"** suicides (an academic paper); **"more than 70"** in the 2009 phase (a movement blog). The widely repeated "1,000-plus martyrs" figure has **no source I could verify**. — [KPIAS summary of the book](https://kpiasacademy.com/telangana-movement-suicides-protests-history/); [IOSR paper](https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jhss/papers/Vol9-issue2/P0902124131.pdf?id=6132)
- **Recommended wording:** "Hundreds of deaths, mostly suicides, are attributed to the movement; published counts range from under 100 to 885, and no independent verification exists. The state government commemorates the dead as *amara veerulu* (martyrs) but has published no audited list."

**Vivid detail:** On 13 February 2014, as the Telangana bill was introduced, Seemandhra MP Lagadapati Rajagopal **sprayed pepper spray inside the Lok Sabha chamber**; the House had to be cleared and several members were taken to hospital. Five days later the bill passed — by voice vote, with the Lok Sabha TV feed blacked out. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telangana_movement)

Commons does hold portraits of K. Chandrashekar Rao and images of the Telangana Martyrs' Memorial, but I did not confirm filenames or licences. **Flagged as an image gap — re-search Commons categories "Telangana movement" and "Telangana Martyrs Memorial" directly.**

## 8. India Against Corruption / The Anna Hazare Movement

**Dates:** 5 April 2011 – December 2011 (active phase); legislative outcome December 2013.

**Where:** Jantar Mantar and **Ramlila Maidan**, New Delhi, with simultaneous protests in Bangalore, Mumbai, Chennai, Ahmedabad and in the diaspora.

**Trigger (plain):** A run of very large corruption scandals — 2G spectrum, the 2010 Commonwealth Games, Adarsh housing — collided with a stalled 42-year-old proposal for an anti-corruption ombudsman. When the government refused to let civil-society representatives help draft the Lokpal bill, Anna Hazare began an indefinite hunger strike. — [Wikipedia: 2011 Indian anti-corruption movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)

**Key dates:**
- **5 Apr 2011** — Hazare begins an indefinite hunger strike at Jantar Mantar. **9 Apr** — the government agrees to a joint drafting committee co-chaired by Pranab Mukherjee and Shanti Bhushan; Hazare ends the fast. **16 Apr** — first committee meeting.
- **4–5 Jun 2011** — Baba Ramdev's separate black-money protest at Ramlila Maidan is broken up in a **midnight police raid** with tear gas and lathi charge; 53 people including 20 police officers treated for injuries. On **29 Aug 2011** the Supreme Court held the Delhi Police responsible for the forcible eviction.
- **16 Aug 2011** — Hazare is **detained before he can begin his second fast and sent to Tihar Jail**; **more than 1,200 supporters** are detained. He begins fasting in custody. The government releases him the same evening; he stays another night because Ramlila Maidan is not ready.
- **19 Aug 2011** — Hazare moves to Ramlila Maidan (capacity 25,000). **21 Aug** — organisers report **over 100,000** at Ramlila Maidan and about **50,000** marching in Mumbai.
- **27 Aug 2011** — **both Houses of Parliament pass a resolution** accepting Hazare's three conditions in principle: a citizens' charter, Lokayuktas in every state, and lower bureaucracy under the Lokpal.
- **28 Aug 2011** — Hazare ends a **12-day, 288-hour fast**.
- **11 Dec 2011** — day-long fast at Jantar Mantar against the Standing Committee's draft. **27–28 Dec 2011** — a fast at Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai, is called off early for poor turnout and ill health; Hazare says the movement is "merely postponed."
— all of the above from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)

**Who led it / how many:** **Anna Hazare** as figurehead; **Arvind Kejriwal**, **Kiran Bedi**, **Prashant Bhushan**, **Shanti Bhushan**, **Santosh Hegde** and **Medha Patkar** on the core committee of India Against Corruption. Crowd figures are organiser-reported and should be labelled as such: over 100,000 at Ramlila Maidan on 21 August 2011 is the most-cited number.

**Demanded:** Enactment of the **Jan Lokpal Bill** — an independent anti-corruption ombudsman with its own investigative machinery, covering the Prime Minister, the judiciary and MPs' conduct in the House, and Lokayuktas in every state.

**WHAT CHANGED:**
- **The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013:** passed by the **Rajya Sabha on 17 December 2013** and the **Lok Sabha on 18 December 2013**; presidential assent **1 January 2014**; in force **16 January 2014**. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokpal_and_Lokayuktas_Act,_2013)
- **But the institution sat empty for five years.** Justice **Pinaki Chandra Ghose** was appointed India's first Lokpal in **March 2019** — sources give 17 March and 23 March 2019; use "March 2019." The Lokpal has since disposed of thousands of complaints but prosecuted almost no senior figure. — [Wikipedia: Lokpal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokpal); [IAS Score](https://iasscore.in/current-affairs/prelims/indias-first-lokpal)
- **The movement produced a political party.** The IAC split over whether to enter electoral politics; Hazare opposed it, Kejriwal went ahead and founded the **Aam Aadmi Party on 26 November 2012**. AAP won 28 of 70 Delhi seats in December 2013, governed with Congress support, and **resigned after 49 days** when it could not pass a Jan Lokpal Bill in the Delhi assembly. It later won Delhi outright in 2015 and 2020, and Punjab in 2022. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)
- **Be honest:** the Act that passed was not the Jan Lokpal the movement demanded, and the ombudsman has been largely toothless. The movement's real legacy is political — it helped end the UPA and launched a party.

**Parliament / legislation link:** Exceptionally strong and unusual: on 27 August 2011 **both Houses passed a "sense of the House" resolution by voice vote in response to a hunger strike outside** — an almost unique instance of Parliament formally answering a street protest. Then the full legislative process, Standing Committee and all, played out over two more years.

**Deaths / violence:** No deaths. The significant violence was the **4–5 June 2011 midnight police raid** on the Ramdev protest at Ramlila Maidan — 53 injured, including 20 police. Ministers said the permission covered a 5,000-person yoga camp, not a 65,000-person political rally; allegations that CCTV footage went missing and that the raid was pre-planned were never resolved. The NHRC sought reports; the Supreme Court blamed the police on 29 August 2011. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)

**Vivid detail:** Hazare refused to leave Tihar Jail after the government ordered his release on 16 August 2011 — he stayed inside and kept fasting until he was allowed to protest at Ramlila Maidan on his own terms. He called it the "second freedom struggle" and urged a *jail bharo* (fill-the-jails) campaign. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)

**Non-partisan note:** The movement drew support from across the spectrum — BJP and CPI(M) both backed the April fast. It was also accused from within of being stage-managed: core member **Prashant Bhushan** later said it had been propped up by the BJP and RSS and that he regretted not seeing it at the time. Include this. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Indian_anti-corruption_movement)

## 9. The Nirbhaya Protests

**Dates:** 16 December 2012 – early January 2013 (acute phase); legislative outcome 3 February / 2 April 2013.

**Where:** **India Gate, Rajpath, Raisina Hill and Jantar Mantar** in New Delhi — protesters tried to reach Rashtrapati Bhavan — plus Kolkata, Bengaluru, Mumbai and dozens of other cities.

**Trigger (plain):** On the night of 16 December 2012 a 23-year-old physiotherapy intern and her male friend boarded an off-duty private bus at Munirka in South Delhi at about 9:30 pm. Six men on board beat the man unconscious and gang-raped and mutilated the woman with an iron rod, then threw them both onto the roadside, where passers-by found them around 11 pm. She died in a Singapore hospital on 29 December. — [Wikipedia: 2012 Delhi gang rape and murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder)

**Key dates:**
- **16 Dec 2012** — the attack. **21 Dec** — thousands at India Gate and Raisina Hill; protesters clash with police and the Rapid Action Force and are baton-charged, hit with water cannon and tear-gassed. **22 Dec** — seven Delhi Metro stations closed to stop people reaching Raisina Hill. **24 Dec** — roads to India Gate and Raisina Hill sealed, nine Metro stations closed, **Section 144 CrPC** imposed (banning gatherings of more than five), curfew near the President's residence; *Hindustan Times* reported **375 tear gas canisters used**.
- **22 Dec 2012** — the **Justice J.S. Verma Committee** is appointed and asked to report in 30 days. **26 Dec** — first meeting, by which point it had already received over 6,000 emails.
- **26–29 Dec 2012** — the victim is flown to Mount Elizabeth Hospital, Singapore, on a Cabinet decision; she dies at **4:45 am Singapore time (2:15 am IST) on 29 December**; cremated in Delhi on 30 December under heavy security.
- **23 Jan 2013** — the Verma Committee submits its report **in 29 days** — a 631-page document in 14 chapters, drawing on about **80,000 public submissions**.
- **3 Feb 2013** — the Criminal Law (Amendment) **Ordinance** is promulgated by President Pranab Mukherjee.
- **19 Mar 2013** — the Criminal Law (Amendment) Bill is **introduced in and passed by the Lok Sabha on the same day**; **21 Mar 2013** — passed by the Rajya Sabha; the ordinance lapsed 4 April 2013.
— from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder); [PRS Bill Track](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-criminal-law-amendment-bill-2013); [Wikipedia: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Law_(Amendment)_Act,_2013); Verma report date and length via [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/justice-verma-committee/) and [ADB Law and Policy Reform](https://lpr.adb.org/resource/report-committee-amendments-criminal-law-verma-committee-report-india)

**Who led it / how many:** **Leaderless** — this is the defining feature. Students, young professionals and women's organisations mobilised by SMS and social media, with no party in charge; political leaders who turned up were booed. Crowd figures were never officially counted; Wikipedia records "thousands" at India Gate and Raisina Hill, **over 600 women** in Bengaluru and "thousands" in a silent march in Kolkata. A protester, Rajesh Gangwar, began a hunger strike on 24 December and kept it up for 14 days. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder)

**Demanded:** The death penalty or chemical castration for rapists; fast-track courts; police accountability; and more broadly, that the state treat sexual violence as a public-order failure rather than a private misfortune. The Verma Committee notably **rejected** the death-penalty demand.

**WHAT CHANGED — substantial:**
- **The Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013** rewrote India's sexual offence law: it widened the definition of rape beyond penile-vaginal penetration; created new offences of **acid attack (326A/326B), sexual harassment (354A), disrobing (354B), voyeurism (354C) and stalking (354D)**; criminalised a public servant's failure to register a rape complaint; and introduced the death penalty for repeat offenders and for rape causing death or a persistent vegetative state. — [Wikipedia: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Law_(Amendment)_Act,_2013)
- Fast-track courts were set up in Delhi — the Delhi High Court approved five, the first inaugurated **2 January 2013** (some sources say six). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder)
- The **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015** replaced the 2000 Act, allowing 16–18-year-olds to be tried as adults for heinous offences — passed directly in response to the release of the case's juvenile offender on 20 December 2015 after three years in a reform home.
- **The executions:** the four surviving adult convicts — Mukesh Singh, Vinay Sharma, Akshay Thakur and Pawan Gupta — were hanged at Tihar Jail at **5:30 am on 20 March 2020**, after four separate death warrants had been issued and stayed. Ram Singh, the bus driver, died in Tihar in March 2013. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder)
- **Be honest about what didn't change.** The Verma Committee's central recommendations were *not* enacted: it recommended **removing the marital-rape exception** (not done — still law in 2026), opposed the death penalty (added anyway), recommended bringing the armed forces under ordinary criminal law for sexual offences (not done), and recommended electoral reform to bar candidates charged with sexual offences (not done). Reported rape cases have risen steadily since 2013 and conviction rates remain around 27–28%.

**Parliament / legislation link:** Textbook, and fast: attack on 16 December, committee on 22 December, report on 23 January, ordinance on 3 February, Act through both Houses by 21 March. **Ninety-five days from protest to statute** — the fastest major law-making response to a protest in this timeline. Also a good case study in the ordinance route versus the bill route, and in how much a bill changes between ordinance and Act (the 2013 Bill differed substantially from the 3 February ordinance).

**Deaths / violence:**
- The victim died on 29 December 2012.
- **Delhi Police constable Subhash Tomar** collapsed during the 23 December protest and died in hospital; the recorded cause was **cardiac arrest**, and whether blunt-force injury caused it was never established. Eight young men were charged with his murder; **police admitted in March 2013 they had no evidence and dropped the charges**. This is the single most important fact-check in the entry — the "protesters killed a policeman" claim circulated widely and was withdrawn. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder)
- Police used **375 tear gas canisters** on 24 December (Hindustan Times, via Wikipedia), water cannon in December cold, and lathi charges; dozens of protesters and police were injured. No protester deaths are recorded.

**Vivid detail:** On 24 December the government **shut nine Delhi Metro stations and sealed the roads to India Gate** to stop people gathering — the seat of government physically closing itself off from the city. Young protesters climbed the lampposts along Rajpath instead; the photographs of them silhouetted against tear gas, Indian tricolour in hand, became the images of the protest. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder); and see the Commons files below, which are literally those photographs.

## 10. The Anti-CAA / NRC Protests and Shaheen Bagh

**Dates:** 4 December 2019 – 24 March 2020.

**Where:** Nationwide. Assam and the Northeast first (for opposite reasons to the rest of the country); then Jamia Millia Islamia and **Shaheen Bagh** in Delhi, Aligarh Muslim University, Mangaluru, Kolkata, Mumbai, Kochi, Hyderabad, Kalaburagi, Malegaon.

**Trigger (plain):** The Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 offered a fast track to Indian citizenship for Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian migrants from Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan who arrived before 2015 — but not Muslims. Critics said that, combined with a proposed nationwide NRC, it made religion a test of citizenship for the first time in Indian law. In Assam the objection was the opposite: that the Act legalised *any* pre-2015 migrant, breaking the Assam Accord's 1971 cut-off. — [Wikipedia: Citizenship Amendment Act protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests)

**Key dates:**
- **4 Dec 2019** — Cabinet clears the bill; protests begin in Guwahati and Dispur, where thousands break police barricades outside the Assam Assembly.
- **9 Dec 2019** — Amit Shah introduces the bill in the Lok Sabha. **10 Dec** — **Lok Sabha passes it, 311–80**. **11 Dec** — **Rajya Sabha passes it, 125–105**. **12 Dec** — presidential assent. **10 Jan 2020** — Act comes into force.
- **15 Dec 2019** — Delhi Police enter **Jamia Millia Islamia** with batons and tear gas; **more than 200 students reported injured**, about 100 detained. The same evening, at **Aligarh Muslim University**, at least 80 students are reported injured.
- **15 Dec 2019** — the **Shaheen Bagh** sit-in begins; it gains national attention on 29 December.
- **22 Jan 2020** — 144 petitions come before a three-judge bench led by CJI S.A. Bobde; the Court issues notice and separates the Assam and Tripura matters.
- **23–27 Feb 2020** — the **North East Delhi riots**.
- **24 March 2020** — Delhi Police clear Shaheen Bagh at **7:30 am**, **101 days** after it began, hours after the national COVID-19 lockdown was announced; **nine people, six of them women, detained**. Jaffrabad, Jamia and Turkman Gate sites cleared the same day.
— from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests); [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/shaheen-bagh-sit-in-protest-cleared-amid-covid-19-lockdown-in-delhi-1502869245.html); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/anti-caa-protesters-removed-from-shaheen-bagh-other-places-60513)

**Who led it / how many:** Leaderless and overwhelmingly young; the defining image was of **Muslim women occupying a road**. Shaheen Bagh was run by local women, many of them grandmothers and housewives who had never protested before, in rotating shifts. Reported crowd sizes for single events:
- Mumbai, August Kranti Maidan (19 Dec): **~20,000**
- Bengaluru (23 Dec): **~80,000**
- Kochi (1 Jan): **50,000–170,000**, one source claiming 500,000
- Hyderabad "Million March" (4 Jan): **100,000+**
- Malegaon, all-women rally (6 Jan): **~50,000**
- Mangaluru (15 Jan): **200,000+**
- Kalaburagi (21 Jan): **300,000+**
— all [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests). These are press and organiser figures; label them as reported, not verified.

**Demanded:** Repeal of the CAA; abandonment of the proposed nationwide NRC and NPR; in Assam, the opposite demand — that the Assam Accord's 1971 cut-off be honoured.

**WHAT CHANGED — honestly, very little:**
- **The Act was not repealed, amended, or stayed.** The Supreme Court refused an interim stay on **20 May 2020**, and refused to stay the **Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2024** when they were notified on **11 March 2024**. — [Supreme Court Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/constitutionality-of-the-citizenship-amendment-act-2019-caa/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/supreme-court-to-hear-pleas-seeking-stay-on-citizenship-amendment-rules-2024-on-march-19-2937878)
- **The challenges are still pending in 2026.** About 200–250 petitions remain tagged together before a bench of **CJI Surya Kant with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V.M. Pancholi**; final hearings were scheduled for **5–7 May 2026**. The Supreme Court Observer case page still showed the matter as **pending** when last updated 22 September 2026, and **I found no reported judgment.** — [SCObserver: hearings scheduled May 2026](https://www.scobserver.in/reports/citizenship-amendment-act-supreme-court-schedules-final-hearings-in-may-2026/); [SCObserver case page](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/constitutionality-of-the-citizenship-amendment-act-2019-caa/) — **re-check this before the site goes live.**
- What the movement did achieve: a nationwide NRC has not been attempted; around a dozen state assemblies passed resolutions against the CAA (symbolic — citizenship is a Union subject); and the CAA sat unimplemented for over four years until the 2024 Rules.
- **The cost was high and fell mainly on the protesters.** Hundreds were charged under the UAPA and sedition laws, many held for years without trial.

**Parliament / legislation link:** Direct — the protest is *about* an Act of Parliament, with a recorded division in both Houses (311–80 and 125–105), which makes it ideal for the site's voting-record feature. Kerala became the first state to sue the Union under **Article 131** over a central law, on 14 January 2020.

**Deaths / violence (disputed — state the range and the claimant):**
- **CAA protests:** Wikipedia's lead cites **27 deaths as of 27 December 2019** while its own infobox lists **"65+"** — the page is internally inconsistent. Most deaths were in **Uttar Pradesh** (at least eight on 19–21 December alone), with others in Assam (Dipanjal Das and Sam Stafford, Guwahati, 12 Dec; Ishwar Nayak, 14 Dec; Abdul Alim, 15 Dec) and Karnataka (two in Mangaluru, 19 Dec). **Recommended wording: "At least 27, and by some counts more than 60, people died in the anti-CAA protests, the great majority of them in Uttar Pradesh; the UP government disputed police responsibility in most cases and no independent national count exists."** Also: 175 injured as of 16 Dec, 3,000+ arrests as of 17 Dec. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests)
- **North East Delhi riots, 23–27 February 2020: 53 killed**, confirmed by Delhi Police on 8 March 2020 and listed in a police affidavit in W.P.(C) 566 of 2020. Breakdown: 36 Muslims, 15 Hindus, 2 unidentified; 44 of the dead were brought to Guru Teg Bahadur Hospital. Over 200 injured. The worst communal violence in Delhi since 1984. **Disputed:** CPI(M) leader Brinda Karat wrote to the Police Commissioner saying at least one death (Sikander, 27 Feb) was omitted. — [Wikipedia: 2020 Delhi riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Delhi_riots); [Outlook](https://outlookindia.com/national/india-news-death-toll-in-last-weeks-delhi-riots-rises-to-53-news-348319); [The Wire](https://thewire.in/communalism/delhi-riots-identities-deceased-confirmed); [Deccan Herald on Karat's letter](https://www.deccanherald.com/national/north-and-central/delhi-riots-death-count-inaccurate-claims-brinda-karat-in-letter-to-police-commissioner-902425)
- **Internet shutdowns:** mobile internet cut in parts of Delhi and Bengaluru on 19 Dec 2019; services suspended in Lucknow, Sambhal, Aligarh, Mau, Ghaziabad, Azamgarh and Dakshina Kannada. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests)

**Vivid detail (several good options):**
- On 29 December 2019 the women of Shaheen Bagh sat out through what was reported as **Delhi's second-coldest night in 100 years**, under tarpaulins, and did not leave. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests)
- On 21 January 2020, more than 100 women at Khureji Khas in Delhi released **10,000 black gas-filled balloons** carrying the message "No CAA NPR NRC." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_Amendment_Act_protests)
- On 19 December 2019, road closures in Delhi delayed **more than 700 flights** and cancelled over 20.
- Shaheen Bagh ended not in a court ruling or a repeal but at 7:30 am on the first morning of the COVID lockdown, when police dismantled the tents and detained nine people, six of them women. — [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/shaheen-bagh-sit-in-protest-cleared-amid-covid-19-lockdown-in-delhi-1502869245.html)

## 11. The Electoral Rolls Protests: SIR and "Vote Chori" (2025–2026) — **ONGOING**

**This is the live entry. Everything here was checked on 27 October 2026 and will move. Build in a "last verified" date and re-check before launch.**

**Dates:** 24 June 2025 – **ongoing** (as of 27 October 2026). The protest phase that is currently in the news began **25 September 2026**.

**Where:** Bihar first; then nationwide as the SIR rolled out in phases. Protests centred on **Delhi — Jantar Mantar, the Election Commission headquarters on Ashoka Road, Parliament Street and the Supreme Court** — with major action in Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata and Patna.

**Trigger (plain):** On 24 June 2025 the Election Commission of India ordered a **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of Bihar's electoral rolls: every voter added since 2003 had to prove eligibility with documents, with the 2003 roll as the baseline. Roughly 47 lakh names came off Bihar's roll. The Commission then extended the exercise nationwide in three phases. The opposition, which had separately been alleging electoral fraud under the slogan **"vote chori"** (vote theft) since August 2025, said the SIR was mass disenfranchisement dressed up as housekeeping. — [Wikipedia: Special Intensive Revision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Intensive_Revision); [Wikipedia: 2025 Indian electoral controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indian_electoral_controversy)

**Parliament / legislation link — unusually rich for this site:**
- **Article 324(5)** of the Constitution: the CEC can be removed only "in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court" — i.e. by an **address of both Houses**. - **Section 18** of the 2023 Act — requires ECI business to be unanimous where possible, by majority otherwise. This is the pleading at the centre of the current petitions.
- The **Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023**, which changed how commissioners are appointed (replacing the CJI on the selection panel with a Union minister) and, per one report, insulates them from legal action.

## 12. BRIEF ENTRY — The Farmers' Protests (2020–21)

*Covered in depth in `farm_laws.md`; include here only as a one-card timeline entry so the decade isn't empty.*

- **Dates:** 26 November 2020 (the "Dilli Chalo" march) – 11 December 2021 (protest suspended). Some sources date the start of the wider struggle to 24 November 2020. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/skm-to-hold-country-wide-protests-on-november-26-to-mark-five-years-of-delhi-march); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/historic-win-one-year-loss-of-lives-farmers-protest-samyukta-kisan-morcha-modi)
- **Where:** The Singhu, Tikri and Ghazipur borders of Delhi; Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.
- **Trigger:** Three farm laws passed in September 2020 (see `farm_laws.md` for the parliamentary record and the Rajya Sabha voice-vote controversy of 20 September 2020).
- **Who:** The **Samyukt Kisan Morcha**, an umbrella of more than 400 farmers' unions. Tens of thousands camped at the borders through two winters and a COVID wave.
- **WHAT CHANGED:** **The laws were repealed.** Modi announced the rollback on **19 November 2021**; the **Farm Laws Repeal Act, 2021** passed both Houses on 29 November 2021 (within minutes, without debate) and received assent on 1 December 2021. The SKM suspended the agitation on **11 December 2021** after written government assurances of **9 December 2021** — on MSP, withdrawal of cases and compensation — which the SKM says in 2026 remain **unfulfilled**. — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/historic-win-one-year-loss-of-lives-farmers-protest-samyukta-kisan-morcha-modi); [Counterview](https://www.counterview.net/2026/08/farmers-to-launch-multi-day-protests.html)
- **Deaths (disputed):** The SKM compiled a list of **702** farmer deaths in December 2021 and later cited **736**; **the government has refused to acknowledge any figure**, telling Parliament it had no record. — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/india/historic-win-one-year-loss-of-lives-farmers-protest-samyukta-kisan-morcha-modi); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/farmers-protest-skm-alleges-govt-solely-responsible-for-present-crisis-casualty-2904398)
- **Also note:** the 26 January 2021 Republic Day tractor parade and the Red Fort incident, and the 3 October 2021 Lakhimpur Kheri killings (8 dead).
- **Images:** abundant and free. Commons returns **138 hits** for "farmers protest Delhi Singhu," including the `Farmers' protest at Singhu Border (15 February 2021)` series and photographs of Maharashtra Adivasi women farmers at Singhu in January 2021. Licences not individually pulled — verify.
