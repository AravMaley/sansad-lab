# Sansad Lab can source almost every number it needs

Sansad Lab has enough citable, mostly primary data to build all five features. The firmest datasets are the **BJP and INC 2024 manifestos**, checked page by page against the PDFs; **PRS bill pages and Vital Stats** for the bill histories and productivity figures; and the **4 June 2024 seat table** (BJP 240, INC 99, NDA 293, INDIA 234, others 16, total 543). Three gaps need attention before launch. First, no AAP, CPI(M) or TMC manifesto position was checked against a primary document, so those parties should stay out of the Manifesto Matcher for now. Second, several dates have small conflicts between sources: farm-law assent (24 vs 27 Sep 2020), Repeal Act assent (30 Nov vs 1 Dec 2021), the CAA Rajya Sabha count (125–105 vs 125–99) and the DPDP Rules gazette date (13 vs 14 Nov 2025). Third, the Lok Sabha has changed a lot since the election. Six Shiv Sena (UBT) MPs merged into Shinde's party, 20 TMC MPs broke away, BJP won a by-election from INC, and at least two seats are vacant. A "current" seat map therefore needs a disclaimer, while the "at result" map does not. The vote records also carry a teaching point. Two of the three landmark bills passed with a recorded division (Women's Reservation 454–2 and 214–0; CAA 311–80 and 125–105). The DPDP Act and the farm laws in the Rajya Sabha passed by **voice vote, with no count at all**. That gap is itself a strong "You Be the MP" lesson.

The coding rule needs to be strict. **"Silent" must not be shown as "opposes."** BJP takes no manifesto position on statements 1, 4, 5, 9, 11 and 12. The asymmetry matters for the user experience. INC takes an explicit position on 11 of the 12 statements, while BJP takes one on about 6. A matcher that scores agreement will therefore tend to match users to INC simply because INC says more. Consider showing "party is silent" as its own visible state rather than scoring it as neutral.

The scenario has a balanced core. The government's case was "one nation, one market." Omar Abdullah's summary, "passed without discussion, repealed without discussion," sets up the scenario's main civic question.

**Women's Reservation** is the near-unanimous case, and it comes with a ready-made sequel. The Act reserves one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha, state assemblies and the Delhi assembly for women, including one-third of SC/ST seats. It lasts 15 years, and it starts only after a post-commencement census and the delimitation based on it ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-constitution-one-hundred-twenty-eighth-amendment-bill-2023)). Supporters point to a demand pending since 1996 and to cross-party consensus. Critics, led by petitioner Jaya Thakur, argue that the census condition indefinitely delays a right ([Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/women-reservation-106-amendment-dr-jaya-thakur-v-union-of-india-wpc-11812023-1564454)). On 17 Apr 2026, the **131st Amendment Bill**, which would have used 2011-census delimitation to apply the quota in 2029, **failed 298–230**. With 528 members present it needed 352 votes for a two-thirds majority. The opposition objected that delimitation would penalise southern states ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/defeat-of-the-constitution-131st-amendment-bill-2026); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/major-setback-modi-govts-constitution-amendment-bill-delimitation-fails-in-lok-sabha)). This makes a good second round to teach how special majorities work. The following are **unverified**: the reason AIMIM voted no (commonly reported as the lack of OBC and Muslim sub-quotas), the absence of an OBC sub-quota, and the 1 Mar 2027 census reference date. A minor conflict: Scroll printed 452 instead of 454 for the Lok Sabha Ayes, and The Quint wrote "over 215" for the Rajya Sabha.

**CAA** is the closest vote, a **20-vote margin** in the Rajya Sabha, so it suits a swing-vote scenario. It fast-tracks citizenship for Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian migrants from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan who entered by 31 Dec 2014. It cuts the residency requirement to 5 years and excludes Sixth Schedule and Inner Line Permit areas ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-citizenship-amendment-bill-2019)). The government framed it as refuge for persecuted minorities that "takes no citizenship away." More than 230 petitions, led by IUML, argue that religion-based eligibility violates Article 14 ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/supreme-court-agrees-to-hear-237-petitions-against-caa-on-tuesday-600866)). Assam protesters cite the 1971 cut-off in the Assam Accord ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_(Amendment)_Act,_2019)). The first certificates were issued on **15 May 2024**, not 14 May as the brief stated ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_(Amendment)_Act,_2019)). One outlet reported the Rajya Sabha vote as 125–99 ([India Legal](https://indialegallive.com/top-news-of-the-day/news/125-105-citizenship-amendment-bill-clears-rajya-sabha/)), but most sources say 125–105. The research found **no report after Feb 2026** on whether the May 2026 hearing took place or whether a verdict was given. Several of the arguments are sourced only to Wikipedia summaries, so check the original wording before quoting them.

**DPDP** is the voice-vote case. It requires consent-based processing with "legitimate use" exceptions, sets up a Data Protection Board, bans tracking and targeted ads aimed at children, and lets the government exempt its own agencies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023)). The fiercest objection is to Section 44(3), which removes the public-interest override from the RTI Act's personal-information exemption ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-refers-pleas-challenging-dpdp-act-amendment-to-rti-act-to-larger-bench-523266)). On 16 Feb 2026 the Supreme Court issued notice, referred the challenges to a larger bench, and said there was "no question of stay" ([IFF](https://www.internetfreedom.in/supreme-court-issues-notice-on-constitutional-challenge-to-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-and-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/)). Later reports on the case's status disagree with each other. The research did **not confirm the widely cited ₹250 crore maximum penalty**, and the PRS DPDP page returned a 404 error, so leave the figure out until it is checked against the Act's Schedule. The Rules gazette date conflicts: 14 Nov 2025 per the Delhi Government OM, 13 Nov per law firms ([Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-implementation-to-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-and-rules/)).

The 18th Lok Sabha's record is uneven. Press citing PRS called it the lowest Lok Sabha productivity since Winter 2016 ([PRS MS 2026](https://prsindia.org/sessiontrack/monsoon-session-2026/vital-stats); [Bhaskar English](https://english.bhaskardigital.com/parliament-session-ends-lok-sabha-productivity-falls-to-15/)). Reported bill counts for that session conflict: 11 according to Outlook, 12 according to Rijiju and Business Standard ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/parliament-monsoon-session-ends-with-12-bills-passed-amid-disruptions-126081400121_1.html)). Before publishing, fetch the full PRS pages for the 2024 sessions and confirm the Rajya Sabha figure for Budget 2025. Always label a figure "PRS" or "Government," because the two methods do not match.

## Conclusion

The research shows that the main risk for Sansad Lab is how it presents the data, not missing data. Three presentation choices could mislead users even when every number is correctly sourced. The first is scoring manifesto "silence" as opposition. The second is mixing PRS and government productivity figures. The third is showing a post-defection seat map as settled when the NCPI merger and the SS(UBT) case are unresolved. Each of these needs an explicit label in the interface. The most distinctive content Sansad Lab can offer may be the vote records themselves. Across the four bills, the cases with the most at stake (the farm laws in the Rajya Sabha and DPDP) left no recorded vote, and the 2026 women's reservation amendment failed only because a special majority was required. Each "You Be the MP" scenario can therefore teach a procedural lesson along with the policy content: how a division differs from a voice vote, why committee referral matters, and why a two-thirds majority is hard to reach.

The fixes before launch are short and specific. Check the BJP's p.55 visually against the PDF. Fetch the e-Gazette entries for the farm-law assent, the Repeal Act and the DPDP Rules. Fetch the sansad.in party-position page and the PRS pages for the 2024 sessions. Confirm the DPDP penalty Schedule. Check the AAP, TMC and CPI(M) stances against their primary documents before adding those parties to the Matcher.
